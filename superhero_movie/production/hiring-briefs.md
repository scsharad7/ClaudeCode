# Hiring Briefs: First 5 Hires

*Ready to post. Before posting, replace each [TBD] from `story/00-my-idea.md` and [producer-plan.md](producer-plan.md). **All rates are rough estimates. Confirm local rates before you make an offer.** Every hire signs a deal memo (a short written agreement) that assigns their work to the LLC.*

**Shared posting header:** *Original superhero proof-of-concept short, [TBD: title], 5–15 min, built around one [TBD: 2–4] minute fight. Shooting in [TBD: city] around [TBD: dates], over 2–5 days. Paid, at a modest rate. The short is the pitch for a feature.*

---

## 1. Producer partner
- **On this project:** runs money, schedule, paperwork, locations and crew logistics so the filmmaker can direct. Also covers line producer and coordinator duties.
- **Must-haves:** has produced at least one finished short; organised with spreadsheets and contracts; local.
- **Where to find:** local film co-ops, 48 Hour Film Project teams, film-school alumni groups, Facebook crew groups.
- **Ask:** "Walk me through a shoot that went wrong and how you fixed it." "How would you split a $15k budget?" "What would you cut first?"
- **Rate (estimate):** small flat fee, roughly $500–$2,000, plus a producer credit and agreed points on the feature.
- **Red flags:** promises investors they can't name; wants feature rights or story control; no finished credits.

## 2. Director of photography (DP)
- **On this project:** designs the look, builds the shot list from the stuntvis cut, and lights the fight so the hits read in wide shots.
- **Must-haves:** a reel with **action or movement** footage; owns or can access a cinema camera package; works well with a stunt team.
- **Where to find:** Vimeo, Staff Me Up, ProductionHUB, Instagram camera-op tags, local rental houses (ask the staff who they rate).
- **Ask:** "How do you keep the geography clear in a fight?" "Show me a scene you shot from a previs." "What can your kit do in 3 days?"
- **Rate (estimate):** roughly $300–$800/day non-union, often with the gear included. Union rates are higher.
- **Red flags:** a reel of only static shots or only shaky-cam; dismisses rehearsal; hidden gear charges.

## 3. Stunt coordinator (non-negotiable)
- **On this project:** designs and supervises all action, runs rehearsals and stuntvis, picks performers, signs off on safety, and briefs the insurer. Often doubles as fight choreographer.
- **Must-haves:** a stunt reel plus **verifiable credits**; a written safety plan or risk assessment on request; current first aid/CPR; working relationships with riggers and medics; willing to be named on the insurance. Check union (SAG-AFTRA) status or references.
- **Where to find:** SAG-AFTRA stunt listings, local stunt teams, martial-arts schools with film credits, stunt-performer Instagram, referrals from DPs.
- **Ask:** "What would you refuse to do on this budget?" "How many rehearsal days does a [TBD]-minute fight need?" "Who's your rigger and medic?"
- **Rate (estimate):** union is around $1,246/day (2025 Basic; confirm the current terms). Non-union rates vary widely. Budget for rehearsal days too.
- **Red flags:** "we don't need rehearsal"; no safety plan; no references; asks you to skip insurance or the medic. **Walk away.**

## 4. VFX lead
- **On this project:** judges which powers are cheap or expensive to show before the script locks, counts the VFX shots, supervises on set (tracking markers, clean plates), and delivers the accents in post.
- **Must-haves:** a compositing reel showing **enhancement, not full CG** (energy, debris, wire removal); fluent in Blender, Fusion/Resolve, Nuke or After Effects; can say no early.
- **Where to find:** ArtStation, r/vfx, Blender communities, local animation schools, Discord VFX servers.
- **Ask:** "Here's the power [TBD]. What's the cheapest way to show it?" "How many shots can you finish in [TBD] weeks?" "What do you need on set?"
- **Rate (estimate):** a flat fee per shot or for the whole job, often a few thousand dollars total for a short. Get a written shot count and price.
- **Red flags:** says yes to everything; no on-set experience; vague delivery dates; no plan for revisions.

## 5. Costume designer
- **On this project:** designs and builds the hero suit [TBD] and villain look [TBD], with **stunt multiples**. The suit must survive falls, pads and sweat.
- **Must-haves:** a portfolio of built costumes (cosplay builders count); experience with foam, armour or stretch fabrics; lead time agreed in writing.
- **Where to find:** cosplay communities, theatre costume shops, Etsy prop and armour makers, fashion and design schools.
- **Ask:** "How long for a hero suit plus one stunt double?" "How will a performer move and fall in it?" "Where do the pads go?"
- **Rate (estimate):** a design fee plus build costs per suit. The total varies a lot with materials; get a quote per suit and per duplicate.
- **Red flags:** no movement test planned; can't commit to a lead time; designs that resemble Marvel or DC suits (an IP risk).
