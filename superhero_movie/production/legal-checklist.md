# Legal & Business-Affairs Checklist: Phases 0–2 and the Short

> **This is not legal advice.** It's a checklist to help you brief a licensed entertainment lawyer, who must confirm every item. Your location is unknown, so it uses **US defaults** and marks rules that differ by state or country with **(varies)**.

Terms: see [GLOSSARY](../GLOSSARY.md) · Background: [production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos) · [story-development §6](../research/story-development.md#legal-risk-dont-drift-too-close-to-marvel-or-dc)

**The goal is a clean chain of title** (the paper trail showing the film's company owns the script, footage, music and performances), so the film can be insured and sold.

---

## Now (weeks 1–2)

- [ ] **Keep dated drafts.** Keep dated backup copies of every draft (git history also counts). Don't rename `00-my-idea.md`, because other files link to it. This is evidence of independent creation, not a registration.
- [ ] **Sign a collaborator agreement before anyone co-develops the story.** Without one, a co-writer may become a **joint author** with equal rights. Cover ownership, credit, any pay, and a later transfer to the LLC.
- [ ] **Originality check.** No Marvel or DC lookalikes: costumes, emblems, colors, names or signature powers. Ideas are free to use, but specific expression is protected.
- [ ] **Keep certain words out of the title.** The joint "Super Hero" marks were cancelled in 2024, but "Super-Villain" marks reportedly remain. Avoid both in the title and marketing anyway, and have the lawyer check.
- [ ] **Run a basic title search** (USPTO trademark search, IMDb, Google). Buy a professional **title report** (a research firm's list of similar titles and trademarks) before you sell the film.
- [ ] **Note any AI-tool use.** Material made purely by AI may not be registrable, so you must disclose it in the copyright application.

## Weeks 7–8: register the script (and week 13 for the short)

- [ ] **US Copyright Office registration (eCO, at copyright.gov).** *Legal copyright protection: needed before you can sue, and registering early preserves statutory damages.* About $45–$65 (confirm). Register **feature draft 2 in week 8**, and the **locked short script at week 13**. Disclose any AI-generated material.
- [ ] **WGA Registry (optional).** *Dated proof that you had the script on a given day. It is not copyright.* Cheap, instant and open to non-members.

## Weeks 9–10: form the company

- [ ] **Form a single-purpose LLC at the start of week 9, before any deal memo** (limited liability company: holds the film's rights and contracts and limits your personal liability). **(varies:** fees and annual taxes differ; California's minimum is $800/year).
- [ ] **Get an EIN** (a free federal tax ID from irs.gov).
- [ ] **Open a business bank account.** Never mix film and personal money.
- [ ] **Sign an operating agreement** (members, decisions, money, exits), even if you're the only member.
- [ ] **Sign a writer assignment** moving both scripts from you to the LLC. This is the first link in the chain of title. **Record the assignment with the Copyright Office.**
- [ ] **Investor documents, if anyone puts money in.** ⚠ **Securities-law warning:** selling a share of profits is usually a "security", and friends-and-family money counts. Mistakes can let investors demand their money back. **Have a lawyer draft the documents before you accept a dollar.** Federal and state filings may apply **(varies)**. Before week 9, accept only gifts or pledges. Ask the lawyer about **fiscal sponsorship** (a nonprofit receives donations for your project, so they're tax-deductible).

## Before pre-production on the short (Phase 3, weeks 11–13)

- [ ] **Deal memos for everyone** (a short contract: job, pay, dates, credit). Include **work-for-hire** language (the work legally belongs to the hirer from the moment it's made) and **rights-assignment** language, so the LLC owns everything each person creates. **(varies)** In California, a written work-for-hire agreement can make the hirer an *employer* for workers' comp purposes.
- [ ] **Releases:**
  - [ ] Cast: performance, name and likeness
  - [ ] Appearance: extras and recognizable passers-by
  - [ ] Location: permission from the owner or manager
  - [ ] Materials: visible artwork, logos, posters, branded products (or blur, replace or avoid them)
- [ ] **Music: any existing song needs two licenses**: **sync** (the composition, from the publisher) and **master** (the recording, from the label). Simpler: **commission an original score** with a rights-assigning composer agreement.
- [ ] **Choose a SAG-AFTRA agreement** if you use union actors. The **Micro tier (under $20k) doesn't allow stunts**, so a union short uses **Short Project (up to $50k)**, and a union short means the **IDEAL (~$34k) tier at minimum**. Apply about a month ahead.
  - [ ] **Confirm with SAGindie** the Short Project salary/deferral terms, and which exhibition (festival, YouTube, crowdfunding video) triggers payment or an upgrade.
- [ ] **Payroll and worker classification.** Employee vs. contractor rules differ by state **(varies)**. Minors need work permits.
- [ ] **Insurance.** The policy must include:
  - [ ] General liability ($1M is the common minimum)
  - [ ] Short-term production package (equipment, props, wardrobe)
  - [ ] **Workers' comp / accident cover for cast, crew and volunteers**, per state rules **(varies)**
  - [ ] **ALL stunts declared in writing**, naming a qualified stunt coordinator. An undeclared stunt may not be covered.
  - [ ] **Who does what:** the line producer (in practice, your producer partner) gets the quotes, the stunt coordinator writes the stunt breakdown for the insurer, and the lawyer reviews the policy. Get an indicative quote with stunts declared *before* locking the budget tier. If it's over ~$1k, the LEAN tier isn't viable.
- [ ] **Location permits** from the film office. Notify police about any visible costumed fights or prop weapons in public **(varies)**.
- [ ] **Weapons and pyro rules.** No weapons in the short. If a prop weapon is added later, declare it to the insurer and ask the stunt coordinator whether an armorer is needed. Live ammunition is never allowed on set. Pyro and squibs need a **licensed pyrotechnician** plus a fire department permit **(varies)**.
- [ ] **Injury waivers.** Have a lawyer review them. They **don't replace insurance or safety**, and they may not hold up in some states.

## Before release

- [ ] **Chain-of-title binder.** One folder holding: copyright certificates, the writer assignment, collaborator agreements, deal memos, releases, music and composer agreements, LLC documents, the title report and the SAG-AFTRA paperwork.
- [ ] **E&O insurance** (errors and omissions: covers claims of infringement or uncleared material). Distributors require it. Buy it when a deal is near, because quotes expire.
- [ ] **Festival and platform delivery.** Check each festival's premiere rules (many reject films already online). Clear music for at least festival use. Only a public release of the finished short (or the full fight) ends festival eligibility. Behind-the-scenes posts, stuntvis clips and a short teaser are fine. Prepare a music cue sheet and a credits list. Confirm you hold online rights before posting to YouTube.
- [ ] **Crowdfunding.** **Rewards** campaigns (Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Seed&Spark) give perks, not profits, so **never promise a profit share**. The money is likely taxable, and you're bound to deliver the rewards. **Equity** crowdfunding (Regulation CF) sells securities through a registered portal and needs a lawyer. For the short, rewards is far simpler, and a rewards campaign to *make* the short works on either release route. Money arrives about 2 weeks after the campaign closes, so don't book deposits against it.

## Templates you'll need

- Collaborator / co-writer agreement
- NDA (non-disclosure agreement), for crew; studios won't sign one
- Writer assignment to the LLC
- LLC operating agreement
- Crew deal memo (work-for-hire)
- Cast deal memo / performer agreement
- Composer agreement
- Releases: cast, appearance, location, materials
- Music sync + master license
- Injury waiver (lawyer-reviewed)
- Crowdfunding rewards terms
- Clearance log (every logo, artwork and song on screen)

## Questions for your one-hour lawyer consult

1. Which state should my LLC be in, and what are the annual costs?
2. Does anyone have a claim to my story?
3. Does my title, or my hero's name, costume or powers, risk resembling existing IP?
4. Which deal memo and release templates should I use, and will you review them?
5. Are my crew employees or contractors here? Do volunteers need workers' comp?
6. What's the simplest compliant structure if friends or family invest?
7. For a fight short: SAG-AFTRA Short Project or non-union? Which exhibitions trigger payment or an upgrade?
8. Will an injury waiver hold up in my state?
9. Do the short's rights carry into the feature? What changes between festivals and online?
10. What will you charge, and what can I do myself?

*Bring this checklist, your dated drafts and a list of everyone involved.*

## Affordable help

- **Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts:** free or low-cost help, with chapters in many states (e.g. NYC VLA, California Lawyers for the Arts).
- **Law school and film school clinics:** supervised students offer free entertainment, IP or small-business help, and many schools share templates.
- **Sundance Institute resources:** Sundance Collab courses and guides (check what's currently offered).
