# Legal & Business-Affairs Checklist: Phases 0–2 and the Short

> **This is not legal advice.** It's a checklist to help you brief a licensed entertainment lawyer, who must confirm every item. Your location is unknown, so it uses **US defaults** and marks rules that differ by state or country with **(varies)**.

Background: [production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos) · [story-development §6](../research/story-development.md#legal-risk-dont-drift-too-close-to-marvel-or-dc)

**The goal is a clean chain of title** (the paper trail showing the film's company owns the script, footage, music and performances), so the film can be insured and sold.

---

## Now (weeks 1–2)

- [ ] **Keep dated drafts.** Put the date in every filename (`2026-10-25_idea.md`) and keep copies in dated cloud storage. This is evidence of independent creation, not a registration.
- [ ] **Sign a collaborator agreement before anyone co-develops the story.** Without one, a co-writer may become a **joint author** with equal rights. Cover ownership, credit, any pay, and a later transfer to the LLC.
- [ ] **Originality check.** No Marvel or DC lookalikes: costumes, emblems, colors, names or signature powers. Ideas are free to use, but specific expression is protected.
- [ ] **Keep certain words out of the title.** Avoid "Super-Villain" and "Super Heroes" in the title and marketing (Marvel and DC reportedly still hold those marks).
- [ ] **Run a basic title search** (USPTO trademark search, IMDb, Google). Buy a professional **title report** before you sell the film.
- [ ] **Note any AI-tool use.** Material made purely by AI may not be registrable.

## Weeks 7–8: register the script

- [ ] **US Copyright Office registration (eCO, at copyright.gov).** *Legal copyright protection: needed before you can sue, and registering early preserves statutory damages.* About $45–$65 (confirm). Register the feature and the short's script.
- [ ] **WGA Registry (optional).** *Dated proof that you had the script on a given day. It is not copyright.* Cheap, instant and open to non-members.

## Weeks 9–10: form the company

- [ ] **Form a single-purpose LLC** (limited liability company: holds the film's rights and contracts and limits your personal liability). **(varies:** fees and annual taxes differ; California's minimum is $800/year).
- [ ] **Get an EIN** (a free federal tax ID from irs.gov).
- [ ] **Open a business bank account.** Never mix film and personal money.
- [ ] **Sign an operating agreement** (members, decisions, money, exits), even if you're the only member.
- [ ] **Sign a writer assignment** moving both scripts from you to the LLC. This is the first link in the chain of title.
- [ ] **Investor documents, if anyone puts money in.** ⚠ **Securities-law warning:** selling a share of profits is usually a "security", and friends-and-family money counts. Mistakes can let investors demand their money back. **Have a lawyer draft the documents before you accept a dollar.** Federal and state filings may apply **(varies)**.

## Before pre-production on the short (Phase 3, weeks 11–13)

- [ ] **Deal memos for everyone** (a short contract: job, pay, dates, credit). Include **work-for-hire** and **rights-assignment** language so the LLC owns everything each person creates.
- [ ] **Releases:**
  - [ ] Cast: performance, name and likeness
  - [ ] Appearance: extras and recognizable passers-by
  - [ ] Location: permission from the owner or manager
  - [ ] Materials: visible artwork, logos, posters, branded products (or blur, replace or avoid them)
- [ ] **Music: any existing song needs two licenses**: **sync** (the composition, from the publisher) and **master** (the recording, from the label). Simpler: **commission an original score** with a rights-assigning composer agreement.
- [ ] **Choose a SAG-AFTRA agreement** if you use union actors. The **Micro tier (under $20k) doesn't allow stunts**, so look at **Short Project (up to $50k)** or **Ultra Low Budget**. Apply about a month ahead and **confirm current terms**.
- [ ] **Payroll and worker classification.** Employee vs. contractor rules differ by state **(varies)**. Minors need work permits.
- [ ] **Insurance:**
  - [ ] General liability ($1M is the common minimum)
  - [ ] Short-term production package (equipment, props, wardrobe)
  - [ ] **Declare ALL stunts, wire work, prop weapons and pyro in writing**, naming a qualified stunt coordinator. An undeclared stunt may not be covered.
  - [ ] Workers' comp per state rules **(varies)**. Volunteers may still count as employees.
- [ ] **Location permits** from the film office. Notify police about any visible costumed fights or prop weapons in public **(varies)**.
- [ ] **Weapons and pyro rules.** Prop weapons need an armorer, and live ammunition is never allowed on set. Pyro and squibs need a **licensed pyrotechnician** plus a fire department permit **(varies)**.
- [ ] **Injury waivers.** Have a lawyer review them. They **don't replace insurance or safety**, and they may not hold up in some states.

## Before release

- [ ] **Chain-of-title binder.** One folder holding: copyright certificates, the writer assignment, collaborator agreements, deal memos, releases, music and composer agreements, LLC documents, the title report and the SAG-AFTRA paperwork.
- [ ] **E&O insurance** (errors and omissions: covers claims of infringement or uncleared material). Distributors require it. Buy it when a deal is near, because quotes expire.
- [ ] **Festival and platform delivery.** Check each festival's premiere rules (many reject films already online). Clear music for at least festival use. Prepare a music cue sheet and a credits list. Confirm you hold online rights before posting to YouTube.
- [ ] **Crowdfunding.** **Rewards** campaigns (Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Seed&Spark) give perks, not profits, so **never promise a profit share**. The money is likely taxable, and you're bound to deliver the rewards. **Equity** crowdfunding (Regulation CF) sells securities through a registered portal and needs a lawyer. For the short, rewards is far simpler.

## Templates you'll need

| Template | When |
|---|---|
| Collaborator / co-writer agreement | Now, if anyone helps develop the story |
| NDA (non-disclosure agreement) | When sharing the script with potential crew (studios usually won't sign one) |
| Writer assignment to the LLC | Weeks 9–10 |
| LLC operating agreement | Weeks 9–10 |
| Crew deal memo (work-for-hire) | Phase 2–3 hires |
| Cast deal memo / performer agreement | Casting |
| Composer agreement | Before scoring |
| Cast, appearance, location and materials releases | Before the shoot |
| Music sync + master license | If using any existing track |
| Injury waiver / assumption of risk (lawyer-reviewed) | Before stunt rehearsals |
| Crowdfunding rewards terms | Before the campaign |
| Clearance log (a spreadsheet of every logo, artwork and song on screen) | During the shoot and edit |

## Questions for your one-hour lawyer consult

1. Which state should my LLC be in, and what are the annual costs?
2. Is my collaborator situation clean? Does anyone have a claim to the story?
3. Can you review the title and the hero's name, costume and powers for resemblance to existing IP?
4. Which deal memo and release templates do you recommend, and will you review mine?
5. Are my crew employees or contractors in this state? Do I need workers' comp even for volunteers?
6. If friends or family want to invest, what's the simplest compliant structure?
7. For a fight short: SAG-AFTRA Short Project or Ultra Low Budget, or non-union?
8. Will my injury waiver mean anything here?
9. What do I need for festivals vs. an online release, and can the short's rights carry into the feature?
10. What will you charge, and what can I do myself?

*Bring this checklist, the logline, the dated drafts and a list of everyone involved so far.*

## Affordable help

- **Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts (VLA):** free or low-cost help for artists, with chapters in many states (for example NYC's VLA and California Lawyers for the Arts). Search "[your state] volunteer lawyers for the arts."
- **Law school clinics:** many law schools run entertainment, IP or small-business clinics where supervised students help for free. Film schools often share referrals and templates too.
- **Sundance Institute resources:** Sundance Collab (online courses, some free) and published guides on legal and business topics. Check what's currently offered.
- **Also:** Film Independent and similar member groups (legal workshops), and your state film office (permit and incentive guidance).
