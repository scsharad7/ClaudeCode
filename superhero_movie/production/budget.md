## 1. Assumptions (edit these first)

| Parameter | LEAN | TARGET | IDEAL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finished short length | 5–8 min | 8–12 min | 10–15 min |
| Fight sequence length on screen | ~2 min | ~3 min | 3–4 min |
| **Nonstop fighting** (actual contact choreography, without breathers or story beats) | ~45–60 sec | ~75–90 sec | ~1.5–2 min |
| Shoot days | **2** (both fight days, with dialogue squeezed in) | **4** (3 fight + 1 drama) | **5** (4 fight + 1 drama) |
| Stuntvis/rehearsal days (before the shoot) | 1 | 2 | 2, plus 2 coordinator prep days |
| Crew on set | ~8 | ~12 | ~15 |
| Cast | 2–3 speaking, plus 2 stunt performers | 3–4 speaking, plus 2 performers | 4–5 speaking, plus 3 performers |
| Union status | All non-union | Non-union (SAG-AFTRA Short Project optional, see §5) | SAG-AFTRA Short Project possible; budget the extra cost |
| Crew pay | Mostly volunteers, with stipends for DP, sound and 1st AD | Keys (heads of department) on reduced flat rates, PAs (production assistants) on stipends | Most crew on reduced day rates |
| Location type | Free or favour location (a friend's warehouse, a dojo) | One paid private location; permit if the city requires one | One paid location with a film permit, plus police notified if visible from the street |
| Food per person per shoot day | $12–15 | $12–15 | $15–20 |
| Contingency | 10% | 10% | 10% (raise to 15% if the VFX shot count grows) |
| Region | US defaults. Rules vary by country and state, and tax incentives rarely apply at this size | | |

**The 2–3 days-per-minute rule.** Research says to budget 2–3 shoot days for each minute of *nonstop* fighting. A 4-minute wall-to-wall fight would therefore need 8–12 days, which none of these tiers can afford. Each tier gets there by keeping nonstop contact to 45 seconds–2 minutes inside a longer sequence padded with breathers, reversals and aftermath. It also budgets at the **bottom** of the rule, which only works with a cut stuntvis and leads who can really fight. If the fight design grows, add days (see the fight table, line 9) rather than squeezing them.

**Insurance note.** Research cites reported premiums of about **$2.5k–$3k** for shoots *without* stunts ([production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos)). Short-term policies for a few days can cost less, but stunts raise the premium and some insurers decline them. Ask a film-insurance broker for quotes **with and without the stunt breakdown** as early as possible. If the quote is higher than your tier's line, take the difference from contingency and then from rows 13, 15 and 5. **Never shoot uninsured.** Most locations will also ask for a certificate of insurance.

**LLC note.** Filing fees vary by state, and some states also charge an annual LLC tax (California's is notably high). Check yours before you form one; see [legal-checklist.md](legal-checklist.md).

## 3. Fight cost: one fight, line by line

This table is **not extra money.** It pulls the fight's share out of the top sheet, so you can see what the fight costs and trade it against everything else. The "Row" column shows which top-sheet row each line comes from. The rates are placeholder estimates; your stunt coordinator replaces them with real ones.

| # | Line | Row | LEAN | TARGET | IDEAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Stunt coordinator: stuntvis and rehearsal | 4 | 1 day × $300 = **300** | 1 day × $350 = **350** | 2 days × $400 = **800** |
| 3 | Stunt coordinator: shoot days | 4 | 2 × $300 = **600** | 3 × $350 = **1,050** | 4 × $400 = **1,600** |

- **Adding a day.** One more fight day costs roughly the per-day burn plus the coordinator, performers and medic. That's about **$1,000 at LEAN, $1,850 at TARGET and $2,700 at IDEAL** (estimates). That is the price of getting closer to 3 days per minute.
- **Insurance stunt loading** sits in row 11 and isn't broken out here. Get that number from the broker quote.
- Stuntvis on phones costs almost nothing, so **never skip it.** It sets the shot list and the day count, and it doubles as pitch material.

## 4. What each tier buys, what it cuts

**Never cut, at any tier:** a qualified **stunt coordinator**, a **medic** on every action day, **insurance with the stunts declared**, **sound** (a production mixer on set plus a sound design pass in post), and **food**. Rehearsal and stuntvis come with the coordinator; never cut them either.

| | LEAN ~$5k | TARGET ~$15k | IDEAL ~$30k |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buys** | One free location, 2 fight days, a coordinator plus 2 performers, a medic, phone stuntvis, a DIY suit with one duplicate, DIY edit and colour, a paid sound designer stipend | One paid location, 3 fight days plus a drama day, a rented cinema camera and lights, a hero suit with a stunt duplicate, a freelance VFX pass, and an editor and composer on stipends | 4 fight days, 3 performers, pro rigging (pull rigs, no flying), a costume designer and 2–3 suit multiples, real breakaways, paid keys, a colourist, ~15–25 VFX accents, and a lawyer consult |
| **Cuts** | Paid crew (mostly volunteers), rentals, a dedicated editor, VFX beyond cleanup, festivals beyond 1–2, any wire work | Most crew pay (stipends), wire work, a colourist, a big festival run | Flying or wire work, pyro or squibs, a second unit, and union crew |
| **Main risk** | The insurance quote alone could take 15–20% of the budget, and 2 days is tight for even 1 minute of fighting | Crew goodwill. Pay something, on time | VFX creep. Lock the shot count before the shoot |

**Recommendation (estimate):** plan at **TARGET**. LEAN is possible only if the leads are trained fighters, the location is free and the insurance quote comes in low.

## 5. SAG-AFTRA implications (confirm current terms)

The figures below come from the research ([fight-scene-design §4](../research/fight-scene-design.md#4-safety-and-legal-essentials-non-negotiable), [production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos)). **Confirm current terms with SAGindie before you choose** (apply about a month ahead).

- **Micro-Budget (≤ $20k) does NOT allow stunts.** By budget size, LEAN and TARGET would otherwise fall into Micro, so a union short with a fight **can't** use it.
- **Short Project (≤ $50k cap)** is the natural fit for the short. Research says it allows stunts under conditions; read those conditions.
- **Ultra Low Budget (≤ $300k cap, ~$257/day from 7/1/2026)** is really for the **feature**.
- **If there are stunts on a SAG-AFTRA low-budget agreement, a stunt coordinator is required, paid at Basic rates (~$1,246/day in 2025).** Here is what that does to row 4 (estimates):

| | LEAN | TARGET | IDEAL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Coordinator days | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| Extra cost vs. this budget's rate | +$2,838 | +$4,480 | +$6,768 |

These figures cover the coordinator only. Union stunt performers, pension and health fringes, and paperwork come on top of that.
- **Recommendation:** shoot the short **non-union** at LEAN or TARGET. Go union only if a specific SAG-AFTRA actor makes the short much stronger, and then budget from IDEAL upward. SAG-AFTRA members generally can't work on non-union projects (Global Rule One), so decide this **before** casting.

## 6. Feature budget topline (later, ranges only)

These are planning ranges only, with no line items until the feature script exists. Sources: [production-roadmap §3](../research/production-roadmap.md#3-budget-tiers) and [GAMEPLAN](../GAMEPLAN.md#budget-at-a-glance).

| Feature tier | Total range (estimate) | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| Micro / low budget | **$50k–$1M** | Paid crew of 10–25, ULB cast (≤ $300k cap), a stunt coordinator, rented cinema cameras, freelance VFX. 3–4 set pieces, mostly practical |
| Indie | **$1M–$10M** | Near-union crew, recognisable cast, a stunt team, a VFX house, tax-incentive locations (*Brightburn* cost about $6M) |

**Fight days drive the feature.** Using the GAMEPLAN escalation (Act 1: 1 night; midpoint: 2–3 days; climax: 3–6 days), plan for **roughly 6–10 fight days** on top of drama days. Re-cost each one with the §3 table, scaled up. The four premium lines are stunts, VFX, suits and insurance. VFX is the most common overrun.

**Decisions needed:**
1. **Budget ceiling / tier.** Options: LEAN ~$5k / TARGET ~$15k / IDEAL ~$30k. *Recommend TARGET*, with LEAN as a fallback only if you have fighter-actors and a free location.
2. **Union or non-union.** Options: all non-union / SAG-AFTRA Short Project / Ultra Low Budget saved for the feature. *Recommend non-union for the short*. If you go union, add +$2.8k–$6.8k for the coordinator alone, and more for performers.
3. **Nonstop fight length vs. shoot days.** Options: trim contact to ~75–90 seconds and shoot 3 fight days / keep 2+ minutes and add 1–2 days (~$1.9k–$2.7k each) / cut the short down to the fight plus a single scene. *Recommend trimming to ~75–90 seconds* inside a ~3-minute sequence.
4. **Where you'll shoot** [TBD: from 00-my-idea.md]. This sets permit, insurance and rate assumptions. Options: a free favour location / one paid private location / a permitted public location. *Recommend one paid private location* that is confined (a warehouse, garage or stairwell).
