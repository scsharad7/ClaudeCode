# Production Roadmap: From "Idea in My Head" to a Superhero Feature

*Research brief for a first-time filmmaker. Budget, location and experience are unknown, so this guide assumes a first-timer and scales across budget tiers. Figures are rough planning ranges, not quotes. Check every legal or financial point with a professional before relying on it.*

---

## TL;DR

- **Don't start with the feature.** Write the feature script, then shoot a **5–15 minute proof-of-concept short** around your best fight.
- **Stunts, VFX, costumes and insurance** are what make superhero films expensive. Write grounded powers and a few big moments.
- **Paperwork comes before cameras** (LLC, chain of title, releases, insurance). A film you can't prove you own can't be sold.
- **Use original characters only.** No Marvel/DC characters, costumes or logos.

---

## 1. The Five Phases of Filmmaking

| Phase | What happens | Key deliverables | Typical indie duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Development** | Turn the idea into a script; secure rights; build the pitch; begin financing | Logline, synopsis, treatment, screenplay drafts, pitch deck, lookbook, top-sheet budget, chain-of-title documents | 3 months to several years (first-timers: plan on 6–12 months) |
| **2. Pre-production** | Lock the script; hire crew; cast; scout and lock locations; schedule; design costumes and sets; rehearse fights; plan VFX | Shooting script, schedule, line-item budget, cast/crew deals, location agreements, insurance, shot lists, storyboards/previs, fight videos | 3–6 months for a feature ([NFI](https://www.nfi.edu/?p=11667)); 4–8 weeks for a short |
| **3. Production** | Principal photography | Daily footage (backed up in three places), call sheets, production reports, script supervisor notes | Indie features often shoot in 18–30 days ([IndieTalk](https://indietalk.com/threads/projected-timeline.1109/post-5738)); a 10-minute short in 2–5 days |
| **4. Post-production** | Edit, VFX, sound design, score, color, mix, deliverables | Picture lock, VFX shots, final mix (stems + M&E), color-graded master, DCP, captions, deliverables package | 6–12 months ([NFI](https://www.nfi.edu/?p=11667)); VFX-heavy films run longer |
| **5. Distribution** | Festivals, sales agent, distributor/streamer deals, self-release, marketing | Trailer, key art, EPK (press kit), festival strategy, E&O insurance, delivery schedule items | 6–24 months after picture lock |

**Rule of thumb:** a first indie feature takes **2–4 years** from idea to audience. A proof-of-concept short takes **6–12 months**.

## 2. Proof-of-Concept Short First (the recommended path)

### Why it works
- **A cheap test.** You learn whether you can direct action and deliver VFX for thousands of dollars, not hundreds of thousands.
- **The best pitch.** Investors and actors will watch 10 minutes. Few will read 110 pages from an unknown writer.
- **It recruits a team and works as a calling card.** Good crew follow finished work. Genre festivals program shorts, and viral shorts lead to feature deals.

### Real shorts that became features

| Short | Feature | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| ***Whiplash*** (2013, ~18 min) | *Whiplash* (2014) | Unable to finance the feature, Damien Chazelle shot one scene for about $23k in 3 days. It won a Sundance jury prize and helped secure the ~$3M feature, which won three Oscars ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/70240), [Filmmaker Magazine](https://filmmakermagazine.com/?p=93323), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiplash_(2013_film))) |
| ***Saw*** (2003, ~9 min) | *Saw* (2004) | Wan and Whannell self-funded one scene from the script. It attracted producers, and Lionsgate bought the feature at Sundance ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw_(film))) |
| ***Lights Out*** (2013, ~3 min) | *Lights Out* (2016) | David F. Sandberg says the short "cost zero dollars." It went viral, became a ~$4.9M Warner Bros. feature that grossed ~$149M, and Sandberg later directed *Shazam!* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lights_Out_(2016_film)), [Indie Film Hustle](https://indiefilmhustle.com/david-f-sandbergs-short-film-lights-out/)) |
| ***Mamá*** (2008, ~3 min) | *Mama* (2013) | The Muschietti siblings made it in Buenos Aires. Guillermo del Toro saw it and executive-produced the feature ([SYFY](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/mama-the-short-film-that-launched-andy-muschietti-career)) |
| ***Alive in Joburg*** (2005–06, ~6 min) | *District 9* (2009) | Neill Blomkamp's mockumentary short caught Peter Jackson's attention. The feature earned ~$210M and a Best Picture nomination ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9)) |
| ***Kung Fury*** | 30-min film (2015) | Kickstarter raised $630,019 from 17,713 backers against a $200k goal. It missed the $1M feature goal and was released on YouTube as a 30-minute film ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kung_Fury), [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2014/01/30475-final-days-high-can-kung-fury-climb/)) |
| ***Code 8*** (2016, ~10 min) — **superpowers** | *Code 8* (2019) + sequel | The Amell cousins launched the short with an Indiegogo campaign that aimed for $200k and raised $2.4M+. Netflix picked up the feature, then the sequel ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_8_(2019_film))) |

**Code 8 is the closest model for this project:** a grounded superpower story, a short-plus-crowdfunding launch, and a streamer pickup.

### Honest recommendation
Write the **full feature script first**, then shoot **one sequence from it**, or a self-contained story set in the same world, at **5–15 minutes**. Build it around:
1. **One great fight or power set piece** (2–4 minutes of action), with
2. **One strong emotional or character beat**, so it doesn't play like a stunt reel, and
3. **A world-building hook** that makes people want the feature.

Keep it under 15 minutes. Festivals program short shorts more easily, and online audiences drop off fast. **Festival-eligibility warning:** many festivals, including Fantasia, reject films that have already been publicly available online ([Fantasia rules](https://asianfilmfestivals.com/2025/10/24/fantasia-international-film-festival-call-for-entry-2026/)). Choose either a festival run first (private Vimeo links for pitching) or a viral YouTube launch tied to crowdfunding, *Code 8*-style. You can't do both.

## 3. Budget Tiers

| Tier | Range | What it realistically buys | Superhero approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No/micro-budget** | < $50k | Volunteer or deferred crew; friends or SAG-AFTRA micro cast; prosumer cameras; free locations; DIY VFX (Blender, Resolve Fusion) | **Ideal for the proof-of-concept short** ($5k–$30k). A feature only with very grounded powers |
| **Low-budget indie** | $50k–$1M | Paid crew of 10–25, ULB cast, a stunt coordinator, rented cinema cameras, freelance VFX, insurance, a real mix | First-feature territory: one or two big set pieces, practical effects plus targeted VFX |
| **Indie** | $1M–$10M | Near-union crew, recognizable cast, a stunt team, a VFX house, tax-incentive locations, a sales agent | *Code 8* ($2.4M+ crowdfunded plus other financing), *Brightburn* (~$6M per its director, [Post Magazine](https://postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2019/May-June-2019/-I-Brightburn-I-director-David-Yarovesky.aspx); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightburn) lists $6–12M) |
| **Studio** | $50M–$250M+ | Stars, thousands of VFX shots, second units, global marketing | Tentpoles often cost $200M+. Don't compete on spectacle |

### What superhero/action costs extra
- **Stunts:** a coordinator, performers, rehearsal weeks, pads and rigging (wire work is expensive), and a medic.
- **VFX:** usually the biggest overrun, priced per shot. **Design powers that are cheap to show:** sound plus lighting, practical wind or debris, aftermath shots, off-screen impacts.
- **Costumes:** each hero suit needs **2–3 duplicates** (stunt, damage, continuity).
- **Insurance:** stunts, guns and pyrotechnics raise premiums ([IndieTalk](https://indietalk.com/goto/post?id=278321)). NYC requires at least $1M general liability, and more for stunts ([FilmLifestyle](https://filmlifestyle.com/film-production-insurance/)).
- **Time and sound:** action takes 3–5 times longer to shoot per page than dialogue, and powers are sold by sound design.

## 4. Full Crew Roster by Department

**Phase key:** D = development, Pre = pre-production, P = production, Post = post-production, Dist = distribution. **Micro column:** what a < $50k production can do. **Keep** means essential. **Combine** means one person can cover it. **Skip** means cut or volunteer.

### Producing
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Producer | Owns the project: money, hiring, schedule, delivery | D | **Keep.** Often the filmmaker plus one organized partner |
| Executive Producer | Brings financing or industry clout | D/Pre | Skip, or credit a major investor |
| Line Producer | Builds and manages the line-item budget day to day | Pre | Combine with producer |
| UPM (Unit Production Manager) | Runs logistics and spending on set | Pre | Combine with line producer |
| Production Coordinator | Paperwork, travel, call-sheet distribution, vendors | Pre | Combine with producer or 2nd AD |

### Writing and Directing
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screenwriter | Writes the script; first link in the chain of title | D | **Keep** (often the director) |
| Director | Creative vision; performances; shot design | D | **Keep** |
| 1st AD | Builds the schedule and runs the set's clock and safety | Pre | **Keep.** The most underrated hire on a first film |
| 2nd AD | Call sheets; cast wrangling; background actors | Pre | Combine with production coordinator |
| Script Supervisor | Continuity and coverage notes, which are critical for fights | Pre (late) | Keep if possible; otherwise combine with an AC or a careful PA |

### Casting
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casting Director | Finds and negotiates with actors; handles agents | Pre | Combine. Self-cast via Backstage or local listings |

### Camera
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Director of Photography (DP) | Look, lighting design, camera choices | Pre | **Keep.** Often owns gear |
| Camera Operator | Operates the camera (often the DP on indies) | P | Combine with DP |
| 1st AC (focus puller) | Focus and camera builds | P | Keep; focus is hard in action |
| 2nd AC | Slate, camera reports, battery and card handling | P | Combine with DIT or a PA |
| DIT / Data Wrangler | Backs up and verifies footage; on-set color | P | Combine with 2nd AC. **Never skip backups** |

### Grip and Electric
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaffer | Head of lighting; executes the DP's plan | Pre (late) | Keep, or merge into a 2-person G&E team |
| Key Grip | Rigging, dollies, camera support, light shaping | Pre (late) | Combine with gaffer |
| Best Boy (electric/grip) | Second-in-command; gear and crew management | P | Skip |

### Art
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production Designer | The film's visual world: sets, palette, hero lair | Pre (early) | Keep. Combine all art roles into one |
| Art Director | Manages the art budget and team | Pre | Combine |
| Set Decorator | Dresses sets | Pre | Combine |
| Props Master | Hand props and weapons (rubber/safety versions) | Pre | Combine with art or a PA |

### Costume, Hair and Makeup
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Costume Designer | Designs and builds the hero/villain suits and duplicates | Pre (early, suits take weeks) | **Keep.** The suit sells the genre |
| Hair and Makeup | Continuity looks; sweat, dirt, bruises | P | Keep one person |
| SFX Makeup | Prosthetics, wounds, creature or power effects | Pre | Combine with HMU, or skip with a simpler design |

### Stunts
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stunt Coordinator | Designs and supervises all stunts; signs off on safety | Pre (early) | **Keep. Non-negotiable for any fight or fall** |
| Fight Choreographer | Designs fights; often films previs "stuntvis" | Pre | Combine with coordinator |
| Stunt Performers / Doubles | Perform the risky action | Pre (rehearsal) | Keep trained performers. Local martial-arts schools and stunt teams are a resource |
| Safety Officer / Set Medic | Medical coverage and risk assessments | P | **Keep a medic on stunt days** |

### VFX and SFX
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| VFX Supervisor | Plans VFX shots; supervises on set (tracking markers, HDRIs, plates) | Pre (early) | **Keep someone in this role**, even a freelance compositor on set |
| Previs Artist | Animatics for complex sequences | Pre | Combine with director or storyboarding |
| VFX Artists | Compositing, CG, clean-up | Post | Freelancers or the director, using Blender, Resolve or After Effects |
| SFX (Practical) Coordinator | Wind, debris, breakaways, squibs, rigs | Pre | Keep for anything pyrotechnic (licensed only); otherwise skip |

### Sound
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production Sound Mixer | Records on-set dialogue | P | **Keep.** Bad sound sinks indie films |
| Boom Operator | Holds the boom mic | P | Combine with mixer on tiny sets |

### Locations
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location Manager / Scout | Finds locations, negotiates contracts, gets permits | Pre | Combine with producer |

### Post-Production
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editor | Shapes the film; cuts fights for impact | Pre (consult) / P (assembly) | **Keep.** Ideally not the director |
| Assistant Editor | Ingest, sync, organization, turnovers to VFX | P | Combine with editor |
| Colorist | Final look and grade | Post | Combine, using DaVinci Resolve |
| Sound Designer | Builds punches, powers, ambience; does Foley | Post | **Keep for superhero work** |
| Re-recording Mixer | Final mix; stems for delivery | Post | Combine with sound designer |
| Composer | Original score and hero theme | Pre/Post | Keep. Emerging composers often collaborate cheaply |
| Music Supervisor | Licenses songs and clears rights | Post | Skip: use original score only |

### Business, Marketing and Support
| Role | Responsibility | Joins | Micro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entertainment Lawyer | Contracts, chain of title, financing documents | D | Keep for one review session; use templates otherwise |
| Production Accountant | Books, payroll, tax-incentive audits | Pre | Use a bookkeeper or spreadsheet; payroll service for paid crew |
| Unit Publicist / Stills Photographer | Behind-the-scenes content, stills, press kit | P | **Keep a BTS shooter.** It fuels crowdfunding |
| Marketing / Distribution Lead | Trailer, socials, festival strategy | Pre onward | Combine with producer |
| Production Assistants (PAs) | Everything: lockups, runs, crafty | P | Keep 2–4 |

**Minimum viable crew for a micro proof-of-concept short (about 10–12 people):** director, producer, 1st AD, DP, 1st AC/DIT, gaffer/grip, sound mixer, production designer/props, costume plus HMU, stunt coordinator plus 2–3 performers, VFX supervisor, a medic on stunt days, and 2 PAs.

## 5. Legal and Business Must-Dos

1. **Form a single-purpose LLC** to limit personal liability, hold rights and contracts, and accept investment.
2. **Chain of title.** The LLC must own everything: the script (via a writer assignment) and all cast and crew work-for-hire agreements. Distributors and E&O insurers audit this.
3. **Copyright the script** with the U.S. Copyright Office. Registration is needed before you can sue, and early registration preserves statutory damages ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/WGA-Register-or-Copyright-my-script), [Script Mag](https://scriptmag.com/features/legally-speaking-it-depends-script-registration)).
4. **WGA Registry** is optional, cheap and instant: proof of date, not copyright ([Writers Store](https://writersstore.com/blogs/news/writers-guild-of-america-west-registration-vs-copyright-registration)).
5. **SAG-AFTRA low-budget agreements:** **Micro-Budget (≤ $20k)**, **Short Project (≤ $50k)** and **Ultra Low Budget (≤ $300k)**, with higher tiers above ([SAG-AFTRA one-sheet](https://sagaftra.org/sites/default/files/sa_documents/low_budget_entertainment_contracts_one_sheet.pdf)). ULB rises to **$257/day from 7/1/2026** ([SAGindie](https://www.sagindie.org/sagaftra/tv-theatrical-contract-2026/)). Apply about a month before the shoot.
6. **Production insurance:** general liability ($1M is the common minimum), equipment and workers' comp. **Declare every stunt, prop weapon and pyrotechnic effect.** No-stunt shoots have reported premiums of about $2.5k–$3k ([IndieTalk](https://indietalk.com/goto/post?id=278321)); stunts cost more.
7. **Location permits** from the film office, plus signed releases from private owners. Notify police if costumed fights or prop weapons will be visible in public.
8. **Releases** for every actor, extra and recognizable person, plus clearance of visible logos and art.
9. **Music** needs both sync and master licenses, so **commission an original score** with a rights-assigning composer agreement.
10. **E&O insurance** is required by distributors. Buy it once a deal is near, since quotes expire quickly ([IndieTalk](https://indietalk.com/goto/post?id=278316)).
11. **IP risk: original characters only.** No Marvel/DC characters, suits, emblems or lookalikes. The USPTO cancelled their joint "Super Hero" trademark in 2024, but marks like "Super-Villain" reportedly remain ([Goodmans](https://goodmans.ca/insights/post/goodmans-ip-blog/super-loss-for-marvel-and-dc---super-hero--trademark-enters-the-public-domain)). Get a title report and have a lawyer check names.

## 6. Funding Options

| Source | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-funding** | The short | Full control. Never fund with debt you can't repay |
| **Friends and family** | Short, micro feature | Written terms and LLC units drafted by a lawyer. Securities laws apply |
| **Crowdfunding** (Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Seed&Spark) | Short, or feature with a short as the pitch | About 5% platform fee plus 3–5% processing ([crowdcrux](https://www.crowdcrux.com/?p=11564)). Kickstarter is all-or-nothing; Seed&Spark releases funds at 80% of goal ([FilmDaft](https://filmdaft.com/crowdfund-your-short-film/)). Genre fans back campaigns (*Kung Fury*, *Code 8*). Budget 10–15% for rewards and fees |
| **Grants / fiscal sponsorship** | Short, development | Fiscal sponsors ([From the Heart](https://fromtheheartproductions.com/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/), Fractured Atlas) make donations tax-deductible. Most grants favor social-issue work over genre |
| **Private equity** | Feature | Investors typically recoup first (often 115–120%), then split profits (often 50/50). Expect to need a deck, a budget and a short |
| **Presales** | Feature ($1M+) | A sales agent sells territories in advance. Needs recognizable cast |
| **Tax incentives** | Feature | Georgia: 20% plus a 10% logo uplift, $500k minimum ([MJCPA](https://www.mjcpa.com/?p=8686)). New Mexico: 25–40%, refundable ([GreenSlate](https://greenslate.com/blog/2025-26-u.s.-film-incentive-hot-spots-top-5-states-at-a-glance)). Paid *after* spending, so you need bridge financing |

**A realistic first-timer stack:** fund the short with your own money, friends and family, or crowdfunding. The short plus the script attract a producer, and the feature is financed with equity, an incentive and presales.

## 7. Distribution Paths

**Festivals for superhero/action genre**
- **[Fantastic Fest](https://fantasticfest.com/film-submission)** (Austin, September): the largest U.S. genre festival, covering horror, sci-fi and action.
- **[Fantasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasia_International_Film_Festival)** (Montreal, July–August): fantasy, action and horror, with many shorts. Requires a Montreal premiere and no prior online release. Submit via [FilmFreeway](https://filmfreeway.com/fantasiafilmfestival).
- **SXSW Midnighters** (Austin, March): the genre midnight section and a career launcher ([THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/sxsw-midnighters-sxfantastic-lineups-unveiled-97760/)).
- Also: Sundance Midnight, TIFF Midnight Madness, Sitges, Overlook, Popcorn Frights.

**Other paths**
- **YouTube premiere:** fast audience-building (*Lights Out*, *Kung Fury*). Best paired with crowdfunding, but it costs you festival eligibility.
- **Sales agents** take the finished feature to buyers at markets (AFM, EFM, Cannes) for a commission plus capped expenses.
- **Streamers and distributors** (Netflix, Shudder, Tubi, Amazon) usually buy through festivals or sales agents.
- **Self-distribution:** TVOD/AVOD platforms and direct-to-fan sales. Keep your backer list.

## 8. First 90 Days: Action Checklist

*Starting point: "I have a beginning, middle and end in my head."*

### Weeks 1–2: Get it out of your head
- [ ] Write a **logline** and a **one-page synopsis** of the beginning, middle and end.
- [ ] Define the hero's **power, limits and cost**, and pick powers that are **cheap to film**.
- [ ] Confirm every character, name and costume is **original**.
- [ ] Watch *Chronicle*, *Brightburn*, *Unbreakable* and *Code 8* (feature and short).
- [ ] Install free screenwriting software (WriterDuet, Fade In or Celtx).

### Weeks 3–6: Write
- [ ] Write a **10–15 page scene-by-scene treatment**.
- [ ] Write the **feature first draft** (90–110 pages, 3–5 pages a day).
- [ ] Mark the **one sequence** that would make the best short.
- [ ] Join a local filmmaker community (film co-op, 48 Hour Film Project, crew groups).

### Weeks 7–8: Protect and plan
- [ ] **Register the copyright** of the draft; WGA Registry optional.
- [ ] Get **3 trusted reads** and revise.
- [ ] Write the **short script** (5–15 pages) from the chosen sequence.
- [ ] Draft a **short budget** (target $5k–$30k) and **a schedule** (2–5 shoot days).
- [ ] Decide: **festival-first or online/crowdfunding-first** for the short.

### Weeks 9–10: Build the core team
- [ ] Recruit a **producer partner**, a **DP** and a **stunt coordinator**. Check reels, and pay something rather than offering "exposure."
- [ ] Recruit a **VFX lead** and a **costume designer** (suits have long lead times).
- [ ] **Form the LLC** and open a bank account.
- [ ] Book a **one-hour entertainment-lawyer consult** to review agreements.

### Weeks 11–12: Pre-production on the short
- [ ] **Stuntvis** the fight: the stunt team films rehearsals on phones.
- [ ] **Cast** the short (consider a SAG-AFTRA Short Project agreement).
- [ ] **Secure locations** with releases and permits.
- [ ] Get **insurance quotes** that declare the stunts.
- [ ] Build the feature's **pitch deck and lookbook**.
- [ ] If crowdfunding: draft the campaign page and pitch video, and start a mailing list.

### Week 13 (Day 90): Checkpoint
- [ ] Feature script draft 2 is done; short script locked.
- [ ] Core team of 4–6 is committed; LLC formed.
- [ ] Short is budgeted and funded (or the campaign is ready to launch), with a shoot date set **6–10 weeks out**.
- [ ] **Next 6 months:** shoot, post (VFX and sound take longest), then submit or launch. After that, pitch the feature with the short in hand.
