## 1. Assumptions (edit these first)

Two terms are used throughout:
- **Fight sequence:** the on-screen length of the whole fight.
- **Nonstop fighting:** continuous contact action, with breathers and story beats left out.

| Parameter | LEAN | TARGET | IDEAL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finished short length | 5–8 min | 8–12 min | 10–15 min |
| Fight sequence | ~2 min | ~3 min | 3–4 min |
| Nonstop fighting | 45–60 sec | ~90 sec | ~2 min |
| **Fight days / drama days / total shoot days** | **2 / 0 / 2** | **3 / 1 / 4** | **4 / 1 / 5** |
| Stuntvis/rehearsal days (before the shoot) | 1 | 2 | 2, plus 2 coordinator prep days |
| Stunt performers | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Rig level (see [stunt plan](../fights/stunt-plan.md#3-risk-level-framework)) | L1–L2 only, no rigs | L1–L2 only, no rigs | L1–L2, plus at most **one L3 pull-rig gag** by professional riggers, declared to the insurer |
| Medic days | 2 shoot + 1 rehearsal = 3 | 3 shoot + 2 rehearsal = 5 | 4 shoot + 2 rehearsal = 6 |
| VFX accent shots | ~3–5 | ~8–12 | ~15–25 |
| Hero suits | 1 + 1 duplicate | 1 + 1 stunt duplicate | 2–3 multiples |
| Crew on set | ~8 | ~12 | ~15 |
| Speaking cast | 2–3 | 3–4 | 4–5 |
| Union status | All non-union | Non-union | Non-union, or a SAG-AFTRA Short Project (see §5; it costs much more) |
| Crew pay (see §2a) | Mostly volunteers, with stipends for DP, sound and 1st AD | Keys (heads of department) on reduced flat rates, PAs (production assistants) on stipends | Keys on reduced day rates, others on stipends, paid through a payroll service if they are employees |
| Location type | Free or favour location (a friend's warehouse, a dojo) | One paid private location, with a permit if the city requires one | One paid location with a film permit, plus police notified if visible from the street |
| Food per person per shoot day | $12–15 | $12–15 | $15–20 |
| Contingency | 10% | 10% | 10% (raise to 15% if the VFX shot count grows) |
| Region | US defaults. Rules vary by country and state, and tax incentives rarely apply at this size | | |

**The days-per-minute rule**
- Budget **2–3 shoot days for each minute of nonstop fighting**.
- **Each extra ~30 seconds of nonstop fighting adds about one fight day** (see the costs in §3).
- Every tier budgets at the **bottom** of the rule. That only works with a cut stuntvis (the fight rehearsed and filmed on phones, then edited before the shoot) and leads who can really move.
- A 4-minute fight sequence of wall-to-wall contact would need 8–12 days. None of these tiers can afford that, so pad the sequence with breathers, reversals and aftermath.

**Insurance note**
- **What's covered.** General liability (GL) covers damage and injury to *third parties*. It does **not** cover your own cast, crew or volunteers. That needs workers' comp or accident cover, so buy both.
- **Before you lock a tier, get an indicative broker quote with the stunts declared.** Research cites about **$2.5k–$3k** for shoots *without* stunts ([production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos)). **If the quote is over ~$1k, LEAN isn't viable.**
- **Who does what:**
  - the **line producer** (in real life, your producer partner) gets the quotes;
  - the **stunt coordinator** writes the stunt breakdown for the insurer;
  - the **lawyer** reviews the policy.
- If the quote is higher than your tier's line, take the difference from contingency and then from rows 13, 5 and 7. **Never shoot uninsured.** Locations will also ask for a certificate of insurance.

**LLC note.** Filing fees vary by state, and some states also charge an annual LLC tax (California's is notably high). See [legal-checklist.md](legal-checklist.md).

## 4. What each tier buys, what it cuts

**Never cut, at any tier:**
- a qualified **stunt coordinator**, with rehearsal and stuntvis;
- a **medic** on every action day and every rehearsal with falls;
- **rated crash mats**;
- **insurance** with workers' comp/accident cover and the stunts declared;
- **sound**: a production mixer on set and a sound design pass in post;
- **food**.

**LEAN (≈ $6.7k)**
- *Buys:* one free location; 2 fight days; a coordinator and 2 performers at floor rates; a medic; rented crash mats; phone stuntvis; a DIY suit with one duplicate; DIY edit and colour; a stipend for the sound designer.
- *Cuts:* paid crew (mostly volunteers), rentals, a dedicated editor, VFX beyond cleanup, any rig.
- *Risk:* viable only if the insurance quote is about $1k or less.

**TARGET (≈ $17.3k)**
- *Buys:* one paid location; 3 fight days plus a drama day; rented camera and lights; a hero suit with a stunt duplicate; a freelance VFX pass; editor and composer stipends.
- *Cuts:* most crew pay (stipends only), any rig, a colourist.
- *Risk:* crew goodwill. Pay something, and pay it on time.

**IDEAL (≈ $33.7k)**
- *Buys:* 4 fight days; 3 performers; one L3 pull-rig gag by pro riggers; 2–3 suit multiples; real breakaways; paid keys through a payroll service; ~15–25 VFX accents; a lawyer consult.
- *Cuts:* flying or wire work beyond the one gag, pyro or squibs, a second unit, union crew.
- *Risk:* VFX creep. Lock the shot count before the shoot.

**Recommendation (estimate):** plan at **TARGET**. Fall back to LEAN only with fighter-actors, a free location and a low insurance quote.

**Location tip:** a confined space (a warehouse or garage) gives clear geography and cheap lighting. **Use a stairwell only with the coordinator's sign-off, and never stage falls on stairs.** Keep all action away from roof edges, ledges and stair drops.

## 5. SAG-AFTRA implications (confirm current terms)

Sources: [fight-scene-design §4](../research/fight-scene-design.md#4-safety-and-legal-essentials-non-negotiable) and [production-roadmap §5](../research/production-roadmap.md#5-legal-and-business-must-dos). **Confirm everything with SAGindie** (apply about a month ahead).

- **Micro-Budget (≤ $20k)** would be the cheapest fit by budget cap, **but it bans stunts.** A union short with a fight therefore uses **Short Project**.
- **Short Project (≤ $50k cap)** "requires a stunt coordinator engaged under the agreement"; confirm this. **Ask SAGindie:**
  - whether Short Project salaries can be **deferred**, and on what terms;
  - which kinds of exhibition (festival, YouTube, a crowdfunding video) **trigger payment or an upgrade** to a higher agreement.
- **ULB (Ultra Low Budget, ≤ $300k cap, ~$257/day from 7/1/2026)** is really for the **feature**.
- **~$1,246/day** (2025 Basic, the main SAG-AFTRA film contract) is the **SAG-AFTRA stunt-performer day minimum**; verify the current rate sheet. A union coordinator is paid at least that much.
- **A union short means the IDEAL tier as a minimum.** On top of IDEAL's total, add (estimates):
  - **coordinator:** 8 days × ~$796 more per day ≈ **+$6.4k**;
  - **stunt performers, if paid at that minimum:** 18 performer-days × ~$1,046 more ≈ **+$18.8k**;
  - plus **fringes** (the union's pension and health contributions, a percentage on top of pay) and paperwork.

  That would bring the short to roughly **$59k–$62k**, which is over the $50k Short Project cap. **It only works if SAGindie confirms you can defer some of that pay.**
- **Recommendation:** shoot the short **non-union**. SAG-AFTRA members generally can't work on non-union projects (Global Rule One), so decide this **before** casting.

## 6. Feature budget topline (later, ranges only)

These are ranges only, with no line items until the feature script exists. Sources: [production-roadmap §3](../research/production-roadmap.md#3-budget-tiers) and [GAMEPLAN](../GAMEPLAN.md#budget-at-a-glance).

| Feature tier | Total range (estimate) | What it implies |
|---|---|---|
| Micro / low budget | **$50k–$1M** | Paid crew of 10–25, ULB cast, a stunt coordinator, rented cameras, freelance VFX. 3–4 set pieces, mostly practical |
| Indie | **$1M–$10M** | Near-union crew, recognisable cast, a stunt team, a VFX house, tax-incentive locations (*Brightburn* cost about $6M) |

**Feature fight days come from the rule, not from a fixed number.**
- Fight days = total minutes of nonstop fighting across the film × 2–3.
- GAMEPLAN's escalation figures (Act 1 about 1 night, midpoint 2–3 days, climax 3–6 days, for **6–10 fight days**) hold only if the three fights add up to about **2.5–4 minutes of nonstop fighting**.
- If they add up to 5 minutes, plan on **10–15 fight days**.
- Re-cost each fight with the §3 table, scaled up. The four premium lines are stunts, VFX, suits and insurance. VFX is the most common overrun.
