# Hiring Briefs: First 5 Hires

*Ready to post, in hiring order. Before posting, replace each [TBD] from [`00-my-idea.md`](../story/00-my-idea.md) and [producer-plan.md](producer-plan.md). **Pay comes from the tier in [budget.md](budget.md). ## 1. Producer partner (post first)
- **On this project:** runs money, schedule, paperwork, locations and crew logistics so the filmmaker can direct. Also covers line producer and coordinator duties, including getting the insurance quotes.
- **Must-haves:** has produced at least one finished short; organised with spreadsheets and contracts; local.
- **Where to find:** local film co-ops, 48 Hour Film Project teams, film-school alumni groups, Facebook crew groups.
- **Ask:** "Walk me through a shoot that went wrong and how you fixed it." "How would you split a $15k budget?" "What would you cut first?"
- **Rate (estimate):** **needs a budget line**, because budget.md doesn't have one yet. It's often deferred pay (paid later, only if the film earns money) plus a producer credit and points (a percentage of the feature's profits), agreed in writing.
- **Red flags:** promises investors they can't name; wants feature rights or story control; no finished credits.

## 2. Stunt coordinator (non-negotiable)
- **On this project:** designs and supervises all action, runs rehearsals and stuntvis (filming the rehearsed fight on phones), picks performers, signs off on safety, and writes the stunt breakdown for the insurer. Often doubles as fight choreographer.
- **Must-haves:** a stunt reel plus **verifiable credits**; a written safety plan or risk assessment on request; current first aid/CPR; their own rated crash mats or a rental source; working relationships with riggers and medics. Check union (SAG-AFTRA) status or references.
- **Where to find:** SAG-AFTRA stunt listings, local stunt teams, martial-arts schools with film credits, stunt-performer Instagram, referrals from DPs.
- **Ask:** "What would you refuse to do on this budget?" "How many rehearsal days does a [TBD]-second stretch of nonstop fighting need?" "Who's your rigger and medic?"
- **Rate (estimate):** per the coordinator lines in [budget.md §3](budget.md#3-fight-cost-one-fight-line-by-line), including rehearsal and prep days. On a union short, the SAG-AFTRA stunt-performer day minimum is ~$1,246/day (2025 Basic); verify the current rate sheet.
- **Red flags:** "we don't need rehearsal"; no safety plan; no references; asks you to skip insurance or the medic. **Walk away.**

## 3. Director of photography (DP)
- **On this project:** designs the look, builds the shot list from the stuntvis cut, and lights the fight so the hits read in wide shots.
- **Must-haves:** a reel with **action or movement** footage; owns or can access a cinema camera package; works well with a stunt team.
- **Where to find:** Vimeo, Staff Me Up, ProductionHUB, Instagram camera-op tags, local rental houses (ask the staff who they rate).
- **Ask:** "How do you keep the geography clear in a fight?" "Show me a scene you shot from previs (a rough rehearsal or animation of the shots made before the shoot)." "What can your kit do in [TBD] days?"
- **Rate (estimate):** per the crew and camera rows in [budget.md](budget.md#2-top-sheet). At Lean that's a stipend using the DP's own kit.
- **Red flags:** a reel of only static shots or only shaky-cam; dismisses rehearsal; hidden gear charges.

## 4. Costume designer
- **On this project:** designs and builds [TBD: if the hero wears a suit / mask] and the villain's look [TBD], with **stunt multiples** (duplicate suits for stunts and damage). Anything the fighters wear must survive falls, pads and sweat.
- **Must-haves:** a portfolio of built costumes (cosplay builders count); experience with foam, armour or stretch fabrics; lead time agreed in writing.
- **Where to find:** cosplay communities, theatre costume shops, Etsy prop and armour makers, fashion and design schools.
- **Ask:** "How long for a hero costume plus one stunt double?" "How will a performer move and fall in it?" "Where do the pads go?"
- **Rate (estimate):** per the wardrobe/suit row in [budget.md](budget.md#2-top-sheet). Get a quote per costume and per duplicate.
- **Red flags:** no movement test planned; can't commit to a lead time; designs that resemble Marvel or DC suits (an IP risk).

## 5. VFX lead
- **On this project:** judges which powers are cheap or expensive to show before the script locks, counts the VFX shots, supervises on set, and delivers the accents in post. On set that means placing tracking markers (small dots the software follows) and shooting clean plates (the same shot without actors, used for cleanup).
- **Must-haves:** a compositing reel showing **enhancement, not full CG** (energy, debris, wire removal); fluent in Blender, Fusion/Resolve, Nuke or After Effects; can say no early.
- **Where to find:** ArtStation, r/vfx, Blender communities, local animation schools, Discord VFX servers.
- **Ask:** "Here's the power [TBD]. What's the cheapest way to show it?" "How many shots can you finish in [TBD] weeks?" "What do you need on set?"
- **Rate (estimate):** per the VFX row in [budget.md](budget.md#2-top-sheet), which is small at every tier. Agree a written shot count and a flat price within that line, or flag that the line needs to grow.
- **Red flags:** says yes to everything; no on-set experience; vague delivery dates; no plan for revisions.
