# Stunt and Safety Plan: Proof-of-Concept Short

> **Read this first.** This plan is a briefing document written by an AI crew agent. It is **not** a substitute for a qualified human stunt coordinator. A real coordinator must review, change and sign off this plan before anyone rehearses or films a single move. Their safety call is final.

**Status:** framework only. [`../story/00-my-idea.md`](../story/00-my-idea.md) and the [fight map](fight-map.md) are blank, so every fight detail is **[TBD]**. Background: [fight research](../research/fight-scene-design.md#4-safety-and-legal-essentials-non-negotiable). **Scope:** one fight in one location, for a $5k–$30k short. US defaults are used here; rules vary by country and state. **Terms:** see [GLOSSARY](../GLOSSARY.md). This plan keeps two lengths apart: the **fight sequence** is the on-screen length of the whole fight, and **nonstop fighting** is only the continuous contact action within it.

**Days, performers and costs** live in [`production/budget.md` §1](../production/budget.md#1-assumptions-edit-these-first), which `film-line-producer` owns. Where this plan repeats a number, it must match that file.

| Tier | Fight sequence | Nonstop fighting | Fight days | Stunt performers | Rig level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LEAN ~$5k | ~2 min | 45–60 s | 2 | 2 | L1–L2, no rigs |
| TARGET ~$15k | ~3 min | ~90 s | 3 | 2 | L1–L2, no rigs |

## 1. Hiring your stunt coordinator

**When:** Phase 2 (weeks 7–10), before the fight is designed, so they can shape it.

**What to look for**
- **Credits** as *stunt coordinator* (not just performer). Check IMDb and ask two directors for references.
- **A reel** showing fights they designed *and* coordinated, ideally on a small budget.
- **Union status:** the SAG-AFTRA **Micro tier bans stunts**, so a union short uses **Short Project** (cap $50k). That agreement requires a stunt coordinator engaged under it, and a union short means the **IDEAL tier at minimum**. Confirm the current terms with SAGindie.
- Experience with **stuntvis**, **ratchet rigs** and **first-time actors**; their own insurance, kit (mats, pads) and performers.

**Where to find one**
- Local stunt teams: search "stunt team reel [your city]".
- Stunt associations: International Stunt Association, Stuntmen's Association, Stuntwomen's Association; the SAG-AFTRA stunt directory if you're union.
- Martial-arts schools whose instructors have film credits; your film commission's crew list.

**Interview questions**
1. Walk me through a fight you coordinated on a budget like mine. What went wrong?
2. How do you run stuntvis, and who cuts it?
3. What's your safety process on the day? Do you bring a medic?
4. What would you cut from my fight to keep it safe and on budget?
5. Can you supply doubles who match my leads?

**What they'll need from you:** the script and fight design doc **[TBD]**; location photos, floor plan, floor surface and ceiling height; cast heights and fitness levels; the schedule and stunt budget; your insurance broker's contact; and suit details (how well it moves, multiples, room for pads).

**Rate ranges (ESTIMATES: confirm with each provider)**

## 2. The action pipeline for the short

Project weeks, assuming a shoot around week 20. Training runs in parallel.

| # | Step | Typical duration | Weeks | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fight design doc (`fight-NN-[TBD].md`) | 1–2 weeks | 11–12 | `film-fight-choreographer` + director; the coordinator reviews |
| 2 | Risk assessment (this plan, filled in) | 2–5 days | 12 | Stunt coordinator (human), with `film-line-producer` on cost |
| 3 | Stuntvis: rehearse it in a gym and film on phones from the planned angles | 1–3 days | 13 | Stunt coordinator + performers |
| 4 | Editor cuts the stuntvis | 1–3 days | 13 | `film-editor` |
| 5 | Revise: director notes, then re-shoot the stuntvis if needed | 1–2 weeks | 14–15 | Choreographer + coordinator + director |
| 6 | Camera plan and shot list taken from the stuntvis cut | 3–5 days | 15–16 | `film-cinematographer` + coordinator |
| 7 | Cast training (starts when casting lands, weeks 11–13) | ≤8 weeks | 12–20 | Choreographer + coordinator |
| 8 | Dress rehearsal on location, in costume, with the camera | 1 day | 19 | Coordinator + `film-first-ad` |
| 9 | Shoot | see below | 20 | `film-first-ad` runs set; the coordinator owns safety |

**Shoot-day math:** allow 2–3 shoot days per minute of *nonstop fighting*. Each extra ~30 seconds of nonstop fighting adds about one fight day. Breathers, dialogue and reactions make the fight sequence longer without adding much shoot time. That's how the tier table above fits 2–4 fight days.

## 3. Risk-level framework

| Level | Examples | Who can do it | People and equipment | Rehearsal | On a micro-budget short? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Choreographed strikes with no contact (cheated hits) or light, controlled contact to padded areas (never the head), blocks, shoves. No weapons. | Trained actors, under the coordinator | Coordinator, medic, mouthguards | 2–5 sessions | **Yes** |
| **L2** | Falls onto mats, ground work, throws, wall hits, balsa and sugar-glass breakaways | Stunt performers or doubles; actors only for simple falls they've trained | Coordinator, performers, medic, rated crash mats, hidden pads, breakaways | Several sessions, plus mats on location | **Yes**: the core of the short. No falls on stairs. |
| **L3** | Ratchet yank (a cable that pulls a performer backwards), jerk vest (a harness that takes the pull), descender (a controlled-lowering device), small wire assist | Professional stunt performers only | Professional riggers, rated hardware, a written rigging plan, a sandbag test (the rig is run with a weighted bag before any person), a dedicated safety person on each line, medic | Rig tests plus full rehearsal on the actual rig | **IDEAL tier only: at most one pull-rig gag**, done by pro riggers and declared to the insurer. LEAN and TARGET use no rigs. |
| **L4** | High falls, vehicle hits, fire or burns, pyro, squibs, firearms, flying wire work | Specialist teams with licences and permits | Airbags, licensed pyro and weapons operators, permits, police | Weeks | **No** |

**Recommendation:** keep the short at **L1–L2**. Only at the IDEAL tier, allow **at most one L3 pull-rig gag** by pro riggers. An L2 signature moment plus good sound usually sells just as well.

## 5. Safety on the day

**Safety meeting script** (the 1st AD and coordinator run it before each new gag, and everyone attends):
1. "Safety meeting for [beat]. The coordinator is [name] and the medic is [name]."
2. Coordinator walks the action through at half speed.
3. "Nobody stands inside [marked area]. Anyone can call **CUT** or **HOLD** for safety; if you hear it, freeze."
4. "Hospital: [name, drive time]. The medic is at [spot]."
5. "If you're not comfortable, say so now. There's no penalty."

**Medic and nearest hospital:** a medic must be present on **every shoot day with action AND every rehearsal with falls**. No medic, no action. Put the nearest emergency room **[TBD]** (address and drive time) on the call sheet, and agree on who drives.

**Pad and mat plan:** the coordinator specifies rated crash mats (rented or from their kit, never borrowed gym mats) for every fall zone, hidden pads under the suit and mouthguards where they won't be seen. Mark the mat placements at the dress rehearsal **[TBD]**.

**Contact rule:** only light, controlled contact to padded areas, as set by the coordinator. Never to the head.

**Stairwells:** only with the coordinator's sign-off, and no falls on stairs.

**Who can call cut:** the director calls cut for creative reasons. **Anyone** can call cut or hold for safety. The coordinator's no can't be overruled.

**Fatigue limits:** the coordinator sets a take limit for each big impact in advance. Shoot the fight early in the week and early in the day, with no complex action late in a long day.

**Resets:** after each take, call "reset", re-check the mats, replace breakaways and check the performers. Roll only when the coordinator calls "stunts ready."

**No-surprises rule:** nothing gets filmed that wasn't rehearsed. Don't add a kick, speed it up or move a camera into the action area until the coordinator has re-walked it.

**The 1st AD's role:** runs the set and schedule, calls the safety meetings, protects rehearsal time, enforces the take limits and clears the action area. When the schedule slips, the AD cuts shots, never safety.

## 6. Insurance declaration checklist

Undeclared stunts can void your cover. You need general liability, equipment cover, and workers' comp or accident cover for cast, crew and volunteers, with **every stunt declared**.

**Who does what:**
- **Stunt coordinator:** writes the stunt breakdown for the insurer, starting from sections 3–4.
- **`film-line-producer`** (in practice, your producer partner): gets the quotes.
- **`film-entertainment-lawyer`:** reviews the policy.

Get an indicative broker quote **with stunts declared** before you lock the tier. If it comes in over ~$1k, LEAN isn't viable.

The insurer will ask for:
- [ ] Every stunt and its risk level (section 4)
- [ ] The coordinator's résumé and union status
- [ ] Which beats the actors do and which the doubles do
- [ ] Fall heights, and any rigging with the riggers' credentials
- [ ] Weapons, pyro, vehicles, minors and animals (the plan is **none**)
- [ ] Location (including any stairwell), dates and number of fight days
- [ ] The medic and the nearest hospital
- [ ] Workers' comp or accident cover, including volunteers (rules vary by state), and the location owner's certificate requirements

## 7. Doubles plan

- **Cast fighters as leads** (martial artists or stunt performers who can act) when you can. You can shoot wider and longer, and the audience sees it's really them.
- **Use actors with doubles** when the drama needs that actor. The actor does L1 and close-ups; the double does L2 and above.
- **Matching:** height within about 2 inches, the same build, matching hair or a wig, and a similar way of moving. Hire a double who matches the performer's gender and ethnicity; don't put a wig on someone to stand in for a different gender, or use make-up to change their skin colour.
- **Masks help a lot:** with a masked hero, a double can do almost everything. You'll need a stunt suit (a spare that can be padded and scuffed), and the actor does the reveal shots. **[TBD: if the hero wears a suit / mask]**.

## 8. Cast training plan

Casting happens in weeks 11–13, so leads get **8 weeks of training at most**, counted from casting. **If the leads aren't trained fighters, give every L2 beat to doubles.** The actors keep L1 and the close-ups.

| When (T = the shoot) | Focus | Sessions |
|---|---|---|
| Casting to T-5 | Fitness, stance, footwork, how to take a cheated hit | 2–3 per week |
| T-5 to T-3 | Learn the choreography phrase by phrase, from the stuntvis | 3 per week |
| T-3 to T-1 | Full speed, selling hits, working with doubles | 3–4 per week |
| Last week | In costume, on location (dress rehearsal), against camera marks | 2–3 sessions |

---

## 9. Never do this on a micro-budget

- Fire, burns, pyro, squibs or explosions
- High falls; any action near a roof edge, ledge or stair drop. No falls on stairs; stairwells only with the coordinator's sign-off.
- Vehicles of any kind: hits, driving stunts, fighting near moving cars
- Weapons of any kind: the short has none. If a prop weapon is added later, declare it to the insurer and ask the coordinator whether an armorer is needed.
- Wire work without professional riggers, or any homemade rig
- Real glass, or furniture that isn't a breakaway
- Choking with real pressure, or any contact to the head
- Water stunts; minors or animals near the action
- Moves invented on the day, or action filmed by exhausted performers
- Fights in public view without permits and without telling the police
