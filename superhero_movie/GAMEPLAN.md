## The strategy

1. **Write the feature script first.** Your beginning, middle and end are the fixed points. The writing team fills in what happens between them.
2. **Then shoot a 5–15 minute proof-of-concept short built around one great fight.** It's the cheapest way to prove you can deliver action, and it's what convinces investors, cast and crew to come on board. *Code 8* followed this path almost exactly: a 10-minute superhero short, then an Indiegogo campaign that raised over $2.4M against a $200k goal, then a feature, then a Netflix sequel. *Whiplash*, *Lights Out*, *Saw* and *District 9* all started as shorts too.
3. **Use the short to fund and staff the feature.**
4. **Every fight is designed from the story, rehearsed and filmed on phones (stuntvis), and cut together *before* anything is shot.** On the day, film real bodies in wide shots and sell the hits with sound. Use CG only to add to or clean up a shot.

Why the short comes first: a superhero feature costs extra in four places (stunts, VFX, suits and insurance). A first-timer who walks in with a finished short gets taken seriously. Someone with only a script usually doesn't. ([research](research/production-roadmap.md#2-proof-of-concept-short-first-the-recommended-path))

## The road map

Timings assume a first-timer working part-time. They come from the [production roadmap research](research/production-roadmap.md#8-first-90-days-action-checklist).

### Phase 0: Shape the idea (weeks 1–2)
- **Goal:** get the film out of your head and onto paper.
- **Crew:** `film-screenwriter`, `film-story-editor`, `film-fight-choreographer`, `film-director`
- **Deliverables:**
  - `story/logline.md`
  - `story/synopsis.md`
  - `story/characters.md` (hero and villain)
  - `story/powers.md` (rules, limits and costs)
  - `fights/fight-map.md` (first pass)
  - `design/director-vision.md` (tone, three reference films, theme)
- **Done when:** you can pitch the film in two sentences. You also know what the hero's power can't do and what it costs them.
- **Homework:** watch *Chronicle*, *Unbreakable*, *Brightburn*, and *Code 8* (both the short and the feature). They show superhero films made on a budget.

### Phase 1: Write the feature (weeks 3–8)
- **Goal:** a first draft of the feature script.
- **Crew:** `film-screenwriter` writes; `film-story-editor` gives notes; `film-fight-choreographer` checks that every fight has stakes, a reversal and a cost; `film-line-producer` gives an early read on what's affordable.
- **Steps:**
  1. Beat sheet (`story/beat-sheet.md`, 15 beats with the fights marked)
  2. Treatment (10–15 pages)
  3. First draft (90–110 pages)
  4. Notes, then draft 2
- **Also:** choose the **one sequence that will become the short**. See *The short's fight* below.
- **Done when:** draft 2 is finished and three people you trust have read it.

### Phase 2: Protect it and build the team (weeks 7–10)
- **Goal:** own the script cleanly and recruit four to six core people.
- **Crew:**
  - `film-entertainment-lawyer`: checklist for the LLC, copyright registration, chain of title, releases and insurance
  - `film-producer`: tier decision and hiring order
  - `film-distribution-marketing`: decide whether the short goes to festivals or online and crowdfunding (see below)
- **Your first real hires:** a producer partner, a DP, a **stunt coordinator**, a VFX lead and a costume designer (suits take a long time to make). Pay people something; don't offer "exposure".
- **Done when:**
  - The LLC is formed.
  - You've had a one-hour consult with an entertainment lawyer.
  - The short script (5–15 pages) is written.
  - The short has a draft budget, with a target of **$5k–$30k**.

### Phase 3: Pre-production on the short (weeks 11–13), then the day-90 checkpoint
- **Goal:** the short is ready to shoot.
- **Crew:**
  - `film-stunt-coordinator` and `film-fight-choreographer`: stunt plan and stuntvis
  - `film-first-ad`: breakdown and schedule
  - `film-line-producer`: budget
  - `film-casting-director`: actors who can move
  - `film-location-manager`: locations
  - `film-costume-designer`: suit
  - `film-cinematographer`: shot list from the stuntvis
- **Day-90 checkpoint:**
  - Feature draft 2 is finished and the short script is locked.
  - The core team is committed.
  - The short is funded, or the crowdfunding campaign is ready to launch.
  - A shoot date is set 6–10 weeks out.
  - Insurance quotes **declare the stunts**.

### Phase 4: Shoot and finish the short (months 4–9)
- **Shoot:** 2–5 days. Schedule the fight early in the week, when everyone is fresh.
- **Post:** edit, sound design, score, VFX accents and colour. VFX and sound take the longest.
- **Crew:** `film-editor`, `film-sound-designer`, `film-composer`, `film-vfx-supervisor`, `film-colorist`

### Phase 5: Launch the short and fund the feature (months 9–15)
- **Two release routes. Choose one, because most festivals won't take films already posted online:**
  - **Festivals first:** Fantastic Fest, Fantasia or SXSW Midnighters. Prestige, sales agents and press.
  - **Online first:** YouTube, timed with a crowdfunding launch (the *Code 8* and *Kung Fury* model). This builds an audience and raises money.
- **Pitch package:** pitch the feature with the short, the script, a look book and the budget topline (`film-producer`, `film-distribution-marketing`).

### Phase 6: The feature (year 2 onward)
Once the feature is funded, work through:
1. Pre-production
2. Production
3. Post-production
4. Distribution

Every crew agent is in play by now. Realistic budget for a first feature: **$50k–$1M**. Brightburn cost about $6M; Chronicle about $12M.

## The fight plan

This is the main focus. The full guide is in [research/fight-scene-design.md](research/fight-scene-design.md).

### The 7 rules (every fight, every time)
1. **Every fight is about something.** Fill in: "This fight is about…". If the story doesn't change when you cut the fight, it isn't finished.
2. **Each fighter wants something specific**, beyond "to win": get the drive, save the hostage, reach the door.
3. **Clear geography.** The audience always knows where everyone is. Confusion reads as boring.
4. **Reversals.** The advantage should change hands every 10–20 seconds.
5. **The hero loses before they win.**
6. **Powers have rules and costs, and the fight exploits them.** Limits create tension; strength alone doesn't.
7. **One signature moment per fight**: the shot that goes on the poster or in the trailer.

### Escalation across the film

| | **Act 1: Opening fight** | **Act 2: Midpoint fight** | **Act 3: Climax fight** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Show the hero's style, limits and power rules | The hero **loses**, and it costs something | The hero wins **by growing**, not just by hitting harder |
| **Feel** | "Special, but raw" | Shock, humiliation, grief | Earned triumph |
| **Scale** | 1–4 opponents, one location, 1–2 min | The villain at full power, 2–3 min, damage | Several phases, 3–6 min, the biggest set piece |
| **Budget** | 1 night, 2–4 stunt performers | 2–3 days, breakaways, 1–2 rigs | 3–6 days, the biggest share of stunt and VFX money |

**The callback trick:** plant a weakness or a move in Act 1, let the villain exploit it in Act 2, and have the hero turn it into the winning move in Act 3.

### How each fight gets made

| Step | What happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Story beat | What the fight is about, the stakes, how it ends | `film-screenwriter` + `film-director` |
| 2. Fight design doc | Beat by beat: reversals, signature moment, environment, safety, VFX, cost (`fights/fight-NN-<name>.md`) | `film-fight-choreographer` |
| 3. Risk & cost | Stunt plan, rigging, rehearsal days, insurance | `film-stunt-coordinator` + `film-line-producer` |
| 4. **Stuntvis** | The stunt team rehearses and films it on phones | stunt team |
| 5. Cut the stuntvis | The editor cuts it so you can watch the fight before you shoot it | `film-editor` |
| 6. Shot list | Camera plan taken from the stuntvis cut | `film-cinematographer` |
| 7. Space & suit | Fight-ready location, breakaways, suits that can move, multiples | `film-production-designer`, `film-costume-designer`, `film-sfx-supervisor` |
| 8. Shoot | Wide shots, real contact (staged safely), stunt coordinator on set, medic | `film-first-ad` runs the day |
| 9. Edit | Let the hits land and vary the rhythm. Faster cutting is not more exciting | `film-editor` |
| 10. Sound | Layered impacts, silence before the big hit, a signature sound for each power | `film-sound-designer` |
| 11. VFX accents | Energy hits, debris, wire removal: add to the shot, don't replace it | `film-vfx-supervisor` |

**Rule of thumb:** budget **2–3 shoot days for each minute of nonstop fighting**.

### The short's fight
Choose **one fight that works on its own**. It should:
- show the hero's power, a clear limit or cost, and their fighting style;
- take place in **one great location**;
- run **2–4 minutes**;
- avoid spoiling the climax.

The Act 1 fight, or a standalone version of the midpoint fight, is usually the right choice. Let `film-fight-choreographer` and `film-producer` recommend one once the fight map exists.

### Non-negotiables
- **Hire a qualified stunt coordinator** for every action day, at any budget. The agents plan the action and help you brief the coordinator; they don't replace one.
- **Rehearse every fight, and keep a medic on set** during action days.
- **Declare all stunts to your insurer.**
- Wire work needs professional riggers. Pyro, squibs and weapons need licensed operators and permits.
- **If you work with SAG-AFTRA:** its Micro tier (under $20k) doesn't allow stunts. For a union short with fighting, plan on the Short Project or Ultra Low Budget agreement and confirm the current terms.

## Budget at a glance

| Tier | Range | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| Proof-of-concept short | $5k–$50k | One great fight, 2–5 shoot days, a small paid core crew |
| Micro / low-budget feature | $50k–$1M | Contained story, 3–4 set pieces, mostly practical effects |
| Indie feature | $1M–$10M | Union cast, a real VFX vendor, bigger set pieces (Brightburn, about $6M) |

Details and what drives superhero costs are in [research/production-roadmap.md](research/production-roadmap.md#3-budget-tiers).

## Decisions only you can make

| Decision | Options | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Path | Short first / straight to feature | **Short first**, built on one fight |
| Short release | Festivals first / online + crowdfunding | Decide by week 8. Online + crowdfunding if you have no industry contacts; festivals if you want sales agents |
| Hero's power | Anything | Choose one with a **clear cost** that's **cheap to film** (strength, speed, energy bursts read well practically) |
| Rating | PG-13 / R | Affects how violent and bloody the fights can be. Decide before writing fight docs |
| Leads | Actors with doubles / actors who can really fight | Look at martial artists and stunt performers for the hero and villain, so you can film wider and longer |
