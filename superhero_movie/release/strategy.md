## 1. The week-8 decision: festivals first, or online + crowdfunding first

**The rule that drives everything:** many genre festivals reject films that are already public online. Fantasia rules out any public online release before or during the festival ([call for entry](https://asianfilmfestivals.com/2025/10/24/fantasia-international-film-festival-call-for-entry-2026/)), and Fantastic Fest has required at least a regional premiere and no public online availability ([FestAgent](https://festagent.com/en/festivals/fantastic_fest)). Private, password-protected Vimeo links for programmers and producers are normally fine.

**What does and doesn't cost you eligibility:**

| Safe on either route | Ends most premiere eligibility |
|---|---|
| Behind-the-scenes clips, stuntvis, training footage, a 30–60 s teaser (check each festival's rules) | Posting the **finished short**, or the full fight, publicly |
| Crowdfunding **to make the short** with a pitch video | |

| | **Festivals first** | **Online + crowdfunding first** |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Prestige, press, laurels, meetings with sales agents, managers and producers | Audience, a mailing list, and money for the feature, fast |
| **Suits you if** | You have (or want) industry access, the short has festival-grade sound and VFX, and you can wait | You have no industry contacts, you can post content every week, and you want to fund the feature directly |
| **Timeline** | 6–18 months from picture lock (deadlines run 4–9 months before each festival), then release online | Launch 1–2 months after lock; the campaign runs 30–45 days |
| **Costs (est.)** | Fees about $40–$90 per festival, so $300–$1,500 for a run, plus travel and a DCP (digital cinema package, the festival screening file; free DCP-o-matic software or a vendor quote) | Platform fee ~5% plus 3–5% processing; rewards and fees 10–15% of the goal ([research](../research/production-roadmap.md#6-funding-options)); optional ad spend; a lot of your time |
| **Premiere rules** | Keep the short offline until the run is over | Most premiere festivals are closed to you afterwards; online-friendly festivals and showcases stay open |
| **Upside** | Validation, reviews, buyers in the room; streamers buy through festivals and sales agents | Proven demand: views, backers and email addresses are numbers that investors understand |
| **Risks** | Rejections (genre shorts sections are very competitive), money spent on fees, and the momentum stalls while you wait | The short goes out to silence if you haven't built an audience; a public miss on an all-or-nothing goal |
| **Real examples** | *Whiplash* (Sundance jury prize, then the feature); *Saw* (short used to win producers, then Lionsgate at Sundance); *Alive in Joburg* (seen by Peter Jackson, became *District 9*) | *Code 8* (short + Indiegogo: $2.4M+ against a $200k goal, then Netflix); *Kung Fury* (Kickstarter: $630k from 17,713 backers, then YouTube); *Lights Out* (viral, then a Warner Bros. feature) |

Sources for the examples: [production-roadmap §2](../research/production-roadmap.md#2-proof-of-concept-short-first-the-recommended-path).

### Recommendation rule
- **If you have no industry contacts and your goal is to fund the feature, go online + crowdfunding first** (the *Code 8* model). Start building the audience now, so it's waiting on launch day.
- **If you already have a producer, manager or agent interested, or the short is clearly festival-grade, go festivals first**, then release online once the run ends.
- **If you're unsure, go festivals first with a time box.** Submit to 5–8 top genre festivals only. If no top festival accepts the short within ~6 months of picture lock, switch to online + crowdfunding. Keep posting BTS content through the wait.

**Planning deadlines**
- Count back from picture lock, currently [TBD: around month 9]. A short locked in summer 2027 fits that autumn's Screamfest, Sitges and Beyond Fest, then SXSW 2028, then Fantasia and Fantastic Fest 2028. Check the **completion-date limits** (e.g., Screamfest ~1 year, Fantasia ~18 months).
- Rank the festivals by **premiere value**. The first big festival gets your world premiere, so don't spend it on a small one.
- Submit at **early-bird** rates. Fees climb $10–$40 per deadline.

- Every submission needs a **"we got in" plan**: the DCP, a press kit, a poster, a trailer, and a budget for at least one trip.

## 3. Online-first plan

**Two campaigns, two jobs.** Keep them separate:
- **A. Fund the short (Phase 3).** Pitch with stuntvis and a pitch video. This works on either route.
- **B. Fund the feature (Phase 5, the *Code 8* move).** Release the short on YouTube on the **same day** the campaign launches, with the campaign link in the first line, the end card and the pinned comment.

**Platform**

| | Kickstarter | Indiegogo |
|---|---|---|
| Model | All-or-nothing: backers aren't charged if you miss the goal | Fixed (all-or-nothing) or flexible (keep what you raise) |
| Fees (est.) | ~5% + 3–5% processing | ~5% + processing; check the flexible-funding terms |
| Precedent | *Kung Fury* | *Code 8* |
| Pick it if | You want urgency and a credible goal | You need flexible funds, or want to keep selling after the campaign (InDemand) |

**Recommendation:** Kickstarter, all-or-nothing, with a goal you can hit from your mailing list alone. Seed&Spark is a film-only alternative ([research](../research/production-roadmap.md#6-funding-options)).

**Campaign timeline**
- **Weeks −8 to −1: pre-launch.** Build the email list (aim for enough sign-ups that 5–10% of them backing covers ~30% of the goal; this is a rule of thumb), line up press and creator partners, and preview the page with 20 friends.
- **Days 1–3: launch.** Email, socials and personal messages to everyone. Aim for ~30% of the goal in 48 hours; momentum attracts strangers.
- **Days ~8–22: the mid-campaign slump.** Plan new content in advance: an exclusive fight-breakdown clip, a stretch goal reveal, a live Q&A with the stunt coordinator, and one update every 3–4 days.
- **Last 72 hours:** countdown emails, "final hours" clips, and a thank-you live stream.

**Reward tiers (examples; keep physical rewards few, since shipping eats margins)**

| Tier (est.) | Reward |
|---|---|
| $10 | Digital thank-you credit, plus early access to the feature's teaser |
| $25 | HD download of the short + a BTS "Making the Fight" featurette |
| $50 | Digital key art + a stuntvis-vs-final comparison video |
| $100 | Signed poster + the fight's choreography breakdown session (live online) |
| $250 | Name in the end credits as "Hero Backer" + a printed suit-design sketch |
| $1,000+ | Set visit or background role (safety-cleared, outside the stunt zone) + an associate producer credit (check the credit terms with the lawyer) |

**Pitch video outline (2–3 min)**
1. A 10 s hook: the best stuntvis or teaser hit.
2. Who you are and why this hero, in one sentence [TBD: logline].
3. The world and the power's rule and cost [TBD: from `story/powers.md`].
4. Proof you can deliver: the team, stuntvis, suit tests.
5. What the money buys, shown as a simple budget split.
6. Rewards and the ask, with the deadline on screen.

## 4. Build the audience from week 1

**Platforms:** TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts (vertical fight clips); a YouTube channel (long-form BTS, then the short); a mailing list (any free tier, e.g. Mailchimp or Kit); Reddit and Discord for the process. Reserve a working handle now and change it once the title is cleared (see the lawyer's title check).

**Comparable audiences to target:** fans of *Code 8*, *Chronicle*, *Brightburn*, *The Boys* and *Invincible*; fight-choreography and stunt-team channels; VFX-breakdown audiences; martial-arts schools near you.

**BTS content calendar (Phases 0–3)**

| Weeks | Phase | Content (2–4 posts/week) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Shape the idea | Intro post ("I'm making an original superhero film"), a mood board without plot reveals, a newsletter sign-up page |
| 3–8 | Write | "Why this fight works" breakdowns of famous fights (the 7 rules), page-count updates, audience polls on power ideas (polls only; you still decide) |
| 7–10 | Team | "Meet the crew" posts: the stunt coordinator, DP and costume designer; suit silhouette sketches |
| 11–13 | Pre-production | **The best content:** fight rehearsal and stuntvis clips, stuntvis vs. storyboard side by side, training montages, suit fittings; the crowdfunding pre-launch if you take route A |

**Rules:** get a signed BTS/appearance release from everyone on camera. **Never post the signature moment in full.** Every post links to the mailing list. The list is the asset you own.

## 5. Key art and trailer

- **Signature moment:** [TBD: from `fights/fight-map.md`]. It's the poster image and the trailer's peak.
- **Key art:** plan a dedicated **poster setup** on the shoot day: the hero mid-signature moment, a strong silhouette, room for the title and laurels. A stills photographer shoots it at high resolution. Deliver a 27×40 poster, plus 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 and 16:9 crops and a YouTube thumbnail.
- **Trailer** (60–90 s), plus 15 s and 30 s vertical cutdowns:
  1. A world hook.
  2. A character beat.
  3. Escalating hits, shown partially.
  4. A tease of the signature moment, cut away before the payoff.
  5. The title, then a sting.

  Don't reveal how the fight ends. Use original music from the composer and the sound designer's power sounds.
- **Press kit (EPK, a pack for festivals and press):** a logline, a synopsis, the director's statement, bios, stills, the poster and a BTS clip.

## 6. If the short gets traction

**Traction signals** (suggested targets, not industry rules): one or more top genre festival selections; or a funded campaign plus a mailing list in the thousands; or a high view count with strong comments and shares.

**Pitch package:** the short, the feature script, the lookbook, the budget topline, and **audience numbers** (views, backers, list size). Build it with `film-producer`.

**Who to approach:**
- **Producers and managers** who scout genre festivals and viral shorts: *Lights Out*, *Mamá* and *Alive in Joburg* were picked up this way.
- **Sales agents** who take features to markets (AFM, EFM, Cannes) and can presell territories.
- **Streamers** (Netflix, Shudder, Tubi, Amazon), which usually buy through festivals or sales agents. *Code 8* went to Netflix.
- **Your backers:** a second campaign for the feature, using the list you built.
