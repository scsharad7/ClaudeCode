01

Thesis

The United States asks skilled immigrants to run a 10–15 year, multi-employer, multi-agency legal process with no system of record, no continuity, and no one accountable for the whole path. Every existing product owns a slice — one filing, one employer, one firm. We own the person's file for the length of their journey.

  1. The unit of value is the case history, not the filing. A filing is a transaction that ends. A person's status history — every I-797, I-94, priority date, travel record, employer change, dependent — compounds in value for a decade and is demanded, in fragments, at every future step. Today it lives in a Gmail folder and a shoebox.
  2. Employer-owned tools structurally cannot hold it. Every HRIS-attached mobility product is scoped to the employment relationship. The moment you change jobs — which the average H-1B holder does two to four times during a 12-year green card queue — the record resets. The need outlives every employer the person will have.
  3. The error cost just went up, which makes correctness purchasable. USCIS has stated it may deny a request outright, without first issuing a Request for Evidence or Notice of Intent to Deny, where initial evidence is missing. First-time-correct used to be cheaper. It is now sometimes the only chance.

What actually goes wrong

  • Nobody holds the timeline. The attorney holds the matter, the employer holds the employment, the person holds the anxiety. No party holds the ten-year path.
  • Documents are scattered; extraction is manual. Receipt numbers, priority dates, I-94 expiry, LCA validity, stamp dates — PDFs and phone photos, re-typed by a paralegal at billable rates, every single time.
  • Advice is expensive and lumpy. Flat fees run $2,000–$15,000 per matter, hourly $150–$500+, consultations $100–$400 — which prices out exactly the questions that are cheap to answer and catastrophic to get wrong ("can I travel in December?").
  • Self-filing is real and badly served. An estimated 50–80% of non-citizens have unmet legal needs. Boundless found RFE rates of 22–29% on marriage-based adjustment; pro se filers skew worse, and the RFE-optional denial policy removes the safety net.
  • The rules move faster than the advice. See section 3.
Assumption

The beachhead pain is coordination and correctness across a long timeline, not cheaper form filling. If the deck's core insight is price disruption of legal fees, section 5 changes materially.

03

Why now — 2026

11.3MCases pending at USCIS, end of FY2026 Q1 — up 17% YoY
6.3MNet backlog, from under 4.3M a year earlier
86Cases completed per 100 received — 11th straight quarter below break-even
12–13 yrEB-2 / EB-3 queue for Indian nationals at current cut-off dates

1. The system is visibly failing at scale. USCIS completed 1.8M cases in Q1, down 41% year over year while receipts kept arriving. A backlog that compounds is a permanent demand signal for tools that help people wait intelligently.

2. Policy volatility became the norm. The $100,000 H-1B fee proclamation was signed 19 Sept 2025, applied from 21 Sept 2025, struck down 8 June 2026, and the First Circuit declined to stay that ruling — leaving employers who budgeted for it and candidates who declined offers over it with a year of decisions made against a rule that no longer exists. Separately, FY2027 replaced the random lottery with wage-weighted selection (Level IV offers get four entries, Level I gets one) against 211,600 registrations for 85,000 slots, a ~40% selection rate. Volatility is the opportunity: a versioned, diff-able policy corpus with a personalised change feed is something no PDF-and-blog incumbent can match.

3. The error cost rose. USCIS's move to deny without first issuing an RFE or NOID converts "complete and correct on first submission" from good practice into the entire game.

4. The technology finally fits. Extraction across messy scans, long-context reasoning over a full history, and retrieval grounded in primary sources are now reliable enough to build on — provided the system answers only from the user's own record plus a cited corpus, and routes everything else to a licensed attorney (section 10).

05

What we're building

Layer 1 — The case graph (the moat)

One structured timeline per person and household: every status, receipt number, priority date, I-94, trip, employer, dependent and deadline, extracted automatically from uploaded documents — I-797, I-94, I-20, EAD, visa stamps, LCA, DS-160 confirmations. This layer is free forever and portable across employers and attorneys. It is the thing no employer-owned tool can hold and no firm has an incentive to hand over.

Layer 2 — The copilot (the daily habit)

Answers grounded in two sources only: your case graph, and a versioned corpus of primary material (USCIS Policy Manual, monthly visa bulletins, the Foreign Affairs Manual, Federal Register notices). Every answer cited to a paragraph and dated. Anything requiring judgment routes to an attorney instead of being guessed at. Plus scenario planning — "if I switch employers in November, what breaks?" — the question people cannot get answered today at any price short of a consult.

Layer 3 — Execution rails (the revenue)

Evidence checklists mapped to the real regulatory criteria for O-1A / EB-1A / NIW; packet assembly; an RFE response builder; an attorney-of-record marketplace with licensed partner firms; and an employer view for the sponsoring side.

Beachhead

Employment-based, high-skilled immigrants in tech, starting with the F-1 → H-1B → O-1/EB-1A/NIW → I-485 chain: urgency, budget, dense referral networks, repeat filings across a decade, and the highest propensity to try a self-serve product before calling a lawyer.

Explicit non-goals for v1

  • Asylum, removal defense, humanitarian relief — different risk, different economics, and doing them badly harms people.
  • Family-based immigration — Boundless owns it, and owns it well.
  • Non-US destinations.
  • Being the attorney. We are the record and the rails; licensed partners are the counsel.
Assumption

All of section 5 is my construction. If the deck defines the product as a marketplace for attorneys, a student advisory service, or an employer compliance tool, sections 5–8 need rewriting — sections 2–4 and 9–10 largely survive.

07

Go-to-market

  1. Community-led, immigrant-first. This audience already congregates around specific creators, subreddits and WhatsApp groups, and trades screenshots of approval notices. Seed where the screenshots are traded. Organic CAC target $40–$80.
  2. University ISSO channel. International student offices are understaffed and legally constrained in what they may advise. A free record-keeping tool for their students is an easy yes, and reaches people at step one of the status chain.
  3. Startup employer channel. Accelerator portfolios — the channel Alma has already validated with YC, Techstars and Pear. Sell the employer tier, acquire the individuals inside it, keep them when they leave.
  4. Attorney partners as a channel, not just supply. Firms hand clients a portal better than their own and receive pre-assembled matters in return.

10

Regulatory, ethical and trust constraints

Not a compliance appendix. In this category these are product architecture.

  1. Unauthorized practice of law. A non-lawyer product giving case-specific legal advice is illegal in every state, and is the failure mode behind decades of notario harm. Architecture: information and document assembly are ours; advice requires a licensed attorney of record; the copilot refuses rather than approximates, and the refusal routes to a human. Get a state-by-state opinion before launch, not after.
  2. Model error in a high-stakes domain. A hallucinated deadline can end someone's status. Retrieval-only answers with dated citations; refusal outside the corpus; human review on anything filed; an eval suite built from real historical cases with known outcomes; a published error-and-correction policy.
  3. Data sensitivity is existential. Immigration status data is among the most sensitive a person can hand over, and in the current climate users are right to be cautious. Commit in writing and in architecture: data minimisation, encryption, defined retention, one-click export and delete, no sale or sharing, and a published policy on responding to government requests. If we are ever the reason someone's data was used against them, no version of this company survives it. Design as though that is the only failure that matters.
  4. Accuracy of policy content. The corpus must be versioned and dated, and every answer must show which version it relied on. The $100k-fee whiplash is the standing example: correct advice in October 2025 was wrong advice by July 2026.

Success metrics

North Star: case milestones completed on time and approved on first submission, per active user, per year. It moves only if the product is used, correct, and consequential.

GuardrailUnresolved hallucination incidents0

Kill criteria. After 100 beta users: if fewer than 25% upload three or more documents in week one, the system-of-record wedge is wrong and we should reconsider being an execution-only service. If partner matters cannot clear 55% gross margin, the marketplace model is wrong and we should be software-only.
